What to Do When a Pediatric Therapy Authorization Runs Out Mid-Treatment: A Parent's Guide

November 11, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Few things feel more jarring than a phone call from the therapy clinic saying, "Your child's authorization has run out, so we can't schedule the next session." Right in the middle of progress, the paperwork catches up with you. Take a breath — this is common, it's usually fixable, and you don't have to untangle it alone. The goal here is simple: help you understand what happened and organize the steps to get coverage flowing again as quickly as possible.

Remember, ClearPath Pediatrics doesn't make clinical decisions or tell you what therapy your child needs — that's your child's licensed providers' role. What we can do is help you understand the navigation side so nothing stalls while the paperwork gets sorted.

First, Confirm What Actually Expired

"Authorization ran out" can mean a few different things, and knowing which one you're dealing with saves time. Gather these details before you call anyone:

  • The authorization number and the exact end date.
  • How many visits were approved versus how many your child has used. Sometimes the visit count is used up before the date; sometimes the date passes first.
  • The service type — physical, occupational, speech, feeding, or behavioral therapy — since each may be authorized separately.
  • Who requested the original authorization: the therapy clinic, your pediatrician, or a specialist.

Your therapy clinic's front desk or billing team can confirm most of this, and your insurer's member services line (on the back of your card) can confirm the rest. Write down the date, time, and name of every person you speak with.

Who Is Responsible for Requesting a New Authorization?

This is the question that trips up the most parents. In most cases, the provider delivering the therapy — or the physician who ordered it — submits the renewal (sometimes called a reauthorization or continuation of care request). It is rarely something a parent files directly. Your job is usually to confirm it's been started and keep it moving.

Call the clinic and ask three plain questions: "Has a reauthorization been submitted?" "What date was it sent?" and "Is there anything you still need from us or from my child's doctor to complete it?" If the request stalls because the clinic is waiting on updated progress notes or a new physician order, that's exactly where a quick nudge from you keeps things from sitting idle for weeks.

Can My Child Keep Attending Therapy While the Renewal Is Pending?

This depends entirely on your plan and your clinic's policies, so ask both directly — don't assume. Some clinics will continue sessions during a pending renewal and bill retroactively once approval comes through; others pause until approval is confirmed to avoid leaving you with an unexpected bill. Ask your clinic: "If we continue sessions while this is pending and the authorization isn't approved, am I financially responsible?" Get the answer in writing if you can. Knowing the financial risk up front lets you make an informed choice rather than being surprised later.

Keep Your Child's Therapy From Losing Momentum

While the paperwork is in motion, a little organization goes a long way:

  • Ask the therapist for a home program. Many will share exercises or activities you can safely do between sessions, so progress doesn't completely stall.
  • Document the gap. Note any days of missed therapy — this can matter if you later need to show continuity of care.
  • Request the progress notes that support ongoing need. These are what the insurer typically reviews for a renewal.
  • Set a follow-up reminder to check status every few business days until you have a decision.

What If the Renewal Is Denied?

A denial is not the end of the road — it's a decision you can question. You have the right to request the specific reason for the denial in writing, and your family has appeal rights spelled out in your plan documents. Your child's provider can often supply additional clinical documentation or request a review. Keep every letter, reference number, and deadline in one place, because appeal windows are time-sensitive.
