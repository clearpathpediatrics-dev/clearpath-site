What Questions to Ask at Your Child's 4-Year Pediatric Well-Child Check

August 30, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric well-child check4 year well visitchild developmental checkupwell-child visit questionspreschool health check

The 4-year well-child check is a busy one. Your child is heading toward preschool or kindergarten, growing fast, and full of new words and big feelings. But these visits move quickly, and it's easy to walk out of the room realizing you forgot half of what you wanted to ask. A little preparation goes a long way. Below are practical questions you can bring with you so the time with your child's provider feels focused instead of rushed.

Remember: this is a list to help you organize the conversation with your own licensed pediatric provider. The answers to what's right for your child come from them.

Questions About Growth and Development

At 4, a lot is changing. Bringing a few targeted questions helps you understand what your provider is watching and how your child is tracking. Consider asking:

  • How is my child's growth trending on their height and weight curves, and what does that mean?
  • Are there any developmental milestones you'd like to keep an eye on before the next visit?
  • How is my child's speech and how they're understood by people outside our family?
  • What does typical play, sharing, and emotional regulation look like at this age?
  • Is there anything you'd suggest I watch for at home and mention next time?

Jot down the answers in the moment. It's easy to think you'll remember, but details blur once you're back in the car with a tired preschooler.

Questions About Vision, Hearing, and Physical Health

The 4-year visit often includes screenings that weren't part of earlier checkups. Understanding what's being done — and why — helps you follow up appropriately.

  • Will my child have a vision or hearing screening today, and how will I get the results?
  • If a screening flags something, what are the next steps and who would we see?
  • Are there any teeth, sleep, or eating patterns I should mention to you or another provider?
  • What immunizations are recommended today, and are any due before kindergarten?
  • Is there anything I should be tracking between now and the next well-child visit?

What Should I Ask About Kindergarten Readiness at the 4-Year Visit?

This is a common question, and it's a great one to raise directly. Ask your provider: "Based on what you're seeing today, is there anything I should address before my child starts school?" Many families use this visit to gather the forms and immunization records schools request. You can ask what paperwork your provider's office can complete, how long it takes, and whether any school or camp physical forms should be filled out now to save a return trip. If your child has any behavior, focus, or communication patterns you've wondered about, this visit is a natural time to mention them so your provider can guide next steps.

How Do I Bring Up a Concern Without Overloading the Visit?

Short visits and long worry lists don't always fit together. The trick is to name your top priorities early. When the provider walks in, try saying: "I have three main things I want to make sure we cover today." That signals what matters most so nothing important gets squeezed out at the end. Write your concerns down in order of priority, and if you run out of time, ask how to follow up — by portal message, a phone call, or a separate appointment. You don't have to solve everything in one visit; you just have to make sure your questions are heard and there's a plan.

Small Prep Steps That Make a Big Difference

Before the appointment, take five minutes to gather what you'll need:

  • A short written list of your top questions and any changes since the last visit.
  • Notes on eating, sleep, mood, or anything that's been on your mind.
  • Your insurance card and any school or camp forms that need completing.
  • A pen or your phone to capture the answers as you go.

Being organized doesn't mean you have to have it all figured out. It just means the time you have with your provider gets spent on your child instead of on scrambling to remember.
