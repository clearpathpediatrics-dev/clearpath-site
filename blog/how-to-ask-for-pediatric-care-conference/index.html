How to Ask for a Pediatric Care Conference: A Parent's Coordination Guide

August 23, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When your child sees several providers, it can feel like everyone holds one piece of the puzzle and no one is looking at the whole picture. A pediatric care conference — sometimes called a care team meeting, case conference, or family meeting — is a scheduled conversation where your child's providers (and you) come together to align on the plan. If you've been wanting everyone in the same room, here's how to actually ask for one.

What a Pediatric Care Conference Is (and Isn't)

A care conference is a coordinated meeting where members of your child's care team share information and agree on next steps. It might include your child's primary pediatrician, one or more specialists, a nurse, a therapist, a social worker, or a school representative — depending on your child's needs. The goal is coordination: making sure everyone is working from the same information and that you understand the plan.

It is not an emergency service, and it is not a place where you'll receive a new diagnosis on the spot. Think of it as a planning and communication meeting. Clinical decisions still belong to your child's licensed providers — the conference simply helps those decisions happen with everyone informed.

How Do I Actually Request a Care Conference?

You can request one directly. Most families start with the provider who knows your child best — often the primary pediatrician or the specialist managing the most active concern. Call the office or send a message through the patient portal, and use clear language like: "I'd like to request a care conference to coordinate my child's plan across their providers. Who can help me set that up?"

Being specific helps. When you reach out, try to include:

  • Why now: a plan that feels unclear, conflicting instructions, an upcoming transition, or a new diagnosis your team is managing.
  • Who you'd like included: name the specific providers or roles.
  • What you hope to leave with: a single written plan, clarity on who owns follow-up, or agreement on next steps.
  • Your availability: a few days and times, and whether phone or video works for you.

Who Can Ask for a Care Conference — and Is There a Cost?

Question: Can a parent request a care conference, or does it have to come from a doctor?

Answer: Yes, parents and legal guardians can absolutely request one. You do not need to wait for a provider to suggest it. You know your child's day-to-day reality better than anyone, and voicing that a conference would help is a completely reasonable request. Some offices have a formal process; others handle it informally. Either way, asking is the first step.

Costs and logistics vary by practice and insurance plan. Some care coordination time may be billable, and coverage differs. It's fair to ask the scheduling office how the meeting will be documented and whether any charges may apply, so there are no surprises.

How to Prepare So the Meeting Is Worth Everyone's Time

A little organization goes a long way. Before the conference, gather your child's current medication list, recent test results, and any instructions that felt confusing or contradictory. Write down your top three to five questions in priority order, since time is often limited. Bring a short summary of what's been happening since the last visit — patterns you've noticed, what's working, and what isn't from your perspective.

During the meeting, take notes or ask if you can record it. Before you hang up, confirm three things: what was decided, who is responsible for each next step, and when you'll follow up. Ask for a written summary if one isn't offered automatically.
