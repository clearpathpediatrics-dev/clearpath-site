How to Ask for a Pediatric Care Conference: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

September 30, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric care conferencepediatric care coordinationcare team meetingmedically complex childparent navigation

When your child sees several specialists, it can feel like everyone knows a piece of the picture and no one is looking at the whole thing. A pediatric care conference — sometimes called a care coordination meeting or family care conference — is a scheduled conversation where your child's providers, and you, sit down together to align on goals, next steps, and who is responsible for what. If you've been thinking "we all just need to be in the same room," you're describing exactly the thing you're allowed to ask for.

The good news: you don't need permission from a specialist to request one. As the parent, you can start the conversation. Below is a practical, word-by-word guide to asking clearly and getting it scheduled.

What Is a Pediatric Care Conference, and When Should You Ask for One?

A care conference is a planned meeting — in person, by phone, or by video — that brings your child's care team together to coordinate. It's useful when several providers are involved, when recommendations seem to conflict, when a hospital stay or new diagnosis has changed things, or simply when you feel like the plan is scattered across too many people.

You might consider asking for one when:

  • Two or more specialists are giving advice that seems to overlap or clash, and you need clarity on who leads what.
  • Your child is transitioning — from hospital to home, from one age of care to another, or between clinics.
  • You're making a big decision and want everyone's input captured in one conversation instead of six separate visits.
  • You feel like you're the only one carrying the full story between appointments.

Who Do You Ask, and How Do You Actually Phrase the Request?

Start with the provider who functions as your child's "home base" — often the primary care pediatrician — or with a care coordinator or case manager if one has been assigned. You can call the office, use the patient portal, or ask at your next visit.

Here's a simple script you can adapt: "My child sees several providers, and I'd like to request a care conference so everyone can coordinate on the plan. Can you help me set that up, or tell me who arranges these? I'd like the primary pediatrician and [names of specialists] included if possible."

Put the request in writing too, even if you've made it by phone. A short portal message creates a record and makes it easier for staff to route. Be specific about why now — "since the recent hospital stay" or "before we decide on next steps" — because a clear reason helps the office prioritize scheduling.

How Do I Ask for a Pediatric Care Conference If Providers Are at Different Clinics?

This is one of the most common hurdles, and it's very askable. When specialists work at different organizations, ask your home-base provider or care coordinator to act as the convener. Request a video or phone conference so no one has to travel, and ask the office to identify a date range that works. You may need to sign release-of-information forms so the different clinics can share records and speak with one another — ask which forms are needed and complete them early, since paperwork is often what slows things down. If no single person will take the lead, a care navigator can help chase down availability across offices.

How to Prepare So the Conference Is Actually Useful

A care conference is only as good as the preparation behind it. Before the meeting, write down your top three to five questions and your goals for your child — what would make the plan feel workable for your family. Bring a current medication list, a one-page summary of your child's history, and any recent recommendations that seem to conflict.

During the meeting, take notes or ask if someone can share a written summary afterward. Before everyone signs off, confirm three things out loud: what was decided, who is responsible for each next step, and when you'll follow up. Ask for the plan in writing through the portal so nothing lives only in memory.

Remember, the goal of a care conference is coordination and clarity — not a place where you make clinical decisions alone. Your licensed providers own the medical recommendations; your job is to make sure they're all working from the same page.
