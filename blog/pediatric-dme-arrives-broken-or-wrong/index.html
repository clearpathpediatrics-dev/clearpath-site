What to Do When Pediatric DME Arrives Broken or Wrong: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

September 19, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

You waited days — maybe weeks — for your child's equipment to arrive. Then you open the box and something is wrong. The wheelchair has a cracked frame, the feeding pump won't power on, the delivered nebulizer is the wrong model, or the sizes are off. It's frustrating and, when the item is something your child relies on daily, genuinely stressful. The good news: this happens often enough that suppliers have clear processes for it, and a calm, documented response usually gets things sorted quickly.

Here is a practical, step-by-step way to handle it — focused on organizing and communicating, not on anything clinical.

First: Don't Use It, and Take Pictures Before You Touch Anything

If equipment looks damaged or clearly isn't what was ordered, set it aside and don't attempt to use it. Before you unpack further or move parts around, take clear photos and a short video: the outside of the box, any shipping damage, the item itself from several angles, the packing slip, and the model or serial number label. Photograph the label on the box too. These images are your strongest evidence and make the supplier's job faster.

Keep all packaging. Suppliers frequently require original boxes and foam inserts for returns or damage claims, and shipping carriers may need to inspect them.

Next: Compare What Arrived to What Was Ordered

Pull out your order paperwork — the delivery ticket, the prescription or order details your provider sent, and any prior authorization document from insurance. Line up the model number, size, quantities, and accessories against what actually arrived. This tells you whether the problem is:

  • A shipping/damage issue (right item, arrived broken)
  • A fulfillment error (wrong model, wrong size, missing parts)
  • An ordering mismatch (what shipped matches the order, but the order itself doesn't match what your provider intended)

Knowing which one you're dealing with points you to the right person to call and speeds up the fix.

How do I report broken or wrong pediatric equipment to the supplier?

Call the DME supplier's customer service number, which is usually on the packing slip or delivery box. State clearly and briefly: your child's name and account or order number, that the item arrived damaged or incorrect, the model number involved, and what specifically is wrong. Ask them to open a case and give you a reference or ticket number. Then follow up with an email that includes your photos and the case number so there's a written record. If your child depends on the item day to day, say so directly and ask about expedited replacement or a loaner while the issue is resolved.

Who do I contact if I'm not sure whether it's a supplier error or an ordering mistake?

Start with the supplier, because they can confirm what was on the order they received. If it turns out the order itself doesn't match what your child needs, loop in whoever wrote the order — your child's provider's office — so a corrected order can be sent. Keep both conversations tied together by referencing the same case number. This is exactly the kind of coordination a care navigator at ClearPath Pediatrics can help you organize when you're juggling a supplier, a provider's office, and an insurance authorization all at once.

Keep a Simple Record While It's Being Fixed

A short log saves you from repeating the story every call. Note the date, who you spoke with, the case number, what they promised, and the expected timeline. Jot down any reference numbers for return shipping labels or replacement orders. If a replacement requires a new insurance authorization, ask the supplier and provider's office who is submitting it, so it doesn't fall through the cracks.

A few things worth confirming out loud:

  • Whether you'll be charged for the return or replacement (you generally shouldn't be for a supplier or shipping error)
  • How the damaged item gets picked up or returned
  • The tracking number and expected delivery date for the corrected item
  • Whether a temporary substitute is available in the meantime

If Your Child Can't Wait

If the equipment is essential and the timeline they offer feels too long, say that plainly and ask to escalate to a supervisor. Ask specifically about loaner or rental options. If a safety concern is involved, that's a conversation for your child's licensed care team — not the supplier — so contact your provider's office or, in an emergency, use emergency services.
