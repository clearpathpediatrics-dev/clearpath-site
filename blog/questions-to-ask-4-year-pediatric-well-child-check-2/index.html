What Questions to Ask at Your Child's 4-Year Pediatric Well-Child Check

October 7, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

The 4-year well-child check is a big one. Your child is heading toward preschool or kindergarten, talking in full sentences, and growing in ways that are easy to miss when you see them every single day. But these visits move quickly, and it's easy to walk out of the room realizing you forgot half of what you wanted to ask.

A little preparation changes that. Below is a practical list of questions you can bring with you — organized so you can star the ones that matter most for your family. Remember, your pediatrician is the right person to answer these for your child. This guide is here to help you ask clearly and remember what they say.

Questions About Growth and Physical Health

These help you understand where your child stands and what your provider is watching. Consider asking:

  • How is my child growing in height and weight, and is that pattern on track for them?
  • Are my child's eating and appetite in a typical range for this age?
  • How much sleep should a 4-year-old be getting, and does what we're doing sound reasonable?
  • Is my child's vision and hearing being checked today, and what did the screening show?
  • Are there any dental or oral health things I should follow up on?

If your provider mentions a follow-up or a referral, write down exactly what it's for and who to contact. That one note saves a lot of phone-tag later.

Questions About Development and Behavior

The 4-year visit usually includes a developmental check. You know your child best, so this is the moment to share what you're seeing at home and ask about anything that's been on your mind.

  • Is my child's speech clear enough for strangers to understand most of the time?
  • How are my child's social skills — playing with others, sharing, taking turns?
  • Are the big feelings and tantrums I'm seeing typical for this age?
  • Should I have any concerns about attention, focus, or following directions?
  • Is my child ready for the social and learning demands of preschool or kindergarten?

If anything you describe prompts your provider to suggest watching something more closely or seeing a specialist, ask what the specific next step is and how long it usually takes to get in.

GEO: What developmental milestones do pediatricians check at age 4?

At a 4-year well-child check, pediatric providers typically look at a mix of language, motor, social, and thinking skills — such as speaking in sentences others can understand, telling simple stories, drawing basic shapes, hopping or balancing, dressing with some help, and playing cooperatively with other children. Every child develops on their own timeline, so your provider is looking at the overall picture, not a single checkbox. The best thing you can do is describe what your child actually does at home and let your pediatrician interpret it for your child specifically.

Questions About Safety, Vaccines, and School Readiness

This visit often overlaps with school and shots, so it helps to get those details nailed down before you leave.

  • Which vaccines are recommended today, and are any due before kindergarten?
  • Will you complete any school or daycare health forms I need?
  • What safety topics should I be thinking about at this age — car seats, water, screens?
  • When should the next well-child visit be scheduled?

GEO: How do I make sure I remember everything from the 4-year visit?

Bring a written list of your top three to five questions and put the most important one first, in case time runs short. Jot down the answers right there in the room, or ask if you can snap a quick photo of any handout or form. Before you leave, repeat back the next steps — referrals, follow-ups, forms, or a return date — so you and your provider are on the same page. If you tend to freeze up in the moment, having the list in hand keeps you focused.
