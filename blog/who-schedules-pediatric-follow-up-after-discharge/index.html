Who Schedules Pediatric Follow-Up Appointments After Discharge (and How to Confirm Them)

September 27, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When your child is discharged from the hospital, the paperwork often lists several follow-up appointments — a check with your pediatrician, maybe a specialist, sometimes a lab or imaging recheck. In the rush of packing up and getting home, it's easy to assume someone else has these handled. Sometimes they do. Sometimes they don't. Knowing exactly who is responsible for scheduling each visit — and how to confirm it — protects your child from gaps that can delay their recovery.

This is one of the most common places things slip after discharge, so it's worth slowing down for a few minutes before you leave the hospital and again once you're home.

Who Actually Schedules Follow-Up Appointments After Discharge?

There isn't one universal answer, and that's exactly why confusion happens. Depending on the hospital and the appointment, responsibility usually falls to one of a few people:

  • The hospital discharge planner or case manager often books certain appointments before you leave — commonly specialist visits or hospital-affiliated follow-ups.
  • The bedside nurse may hand you printed instructions listing appointments that are already made, along with dates, times, and locations.
  • You, the parent, may be asked to call and schedule some visits yourself — especially with your regular pediatrician or an outside specialist not connected to the hospital.
  • The referring specialist's office may reach out to you to set a date, or expect you to call them.

The key is that some appointments are made for you and some are left to you — and the discharge paperwork doesn't always make the split obvious.

How Do I Know Which Appointments Are Already Booked?

Before you leave the hospital, ask the person handing you your discharge instructions to go through the follow-up list line by line with you. For each appointment, get a clear answer to three things: Is this already scheduled, or do I need to book it? If it's scheduled, what is the exact date, time, and location? If I need to book it, who do I call and by when?

Write the answers directly on your discharge paperwork or in your phone. A visit that says "follow up with cardiology in 1 week" is not the same as a confirmed appointment — that wording usually means you need to make it happen. When in doubt, assume it is not booked until someone confirms a real date and time.

How to Confirm Each Appointment Once You're Home

Confirming is a short, specific process. Within a day or two of getting home, call each office on your list and verify:

  • That the appointment exists in their system under your child's name and date of birth
  • The exact date, time, and address (some practices have multiple locations)
  • Whether a referral or prior authorization is needed, and whether it's already on file
  • What to bring — discharge summary, imaging discs, medication list, insurance card

For appointments you were told to schedule yourself, call promptly. Popular specialists book out quickly, and the window your child's care team recommended matters. If you can't get a date that fits the timeframe you were given, ask the scheduler to note that it's a post-discharge follow-up — that phrase sometimes opens up earlier slots.

What If Two Offices Point at Each Other?

This happens more than you'd expect: the hospital says the specialist will call you, and the specialist says they're waiting on the hospital's referral. If you hit that loop, don't keep bouncing between them. Call the hospital's discharge planning or case management line, explain that an appointment isn't getting scheduled, and ask them to close the gap. Keep a simple log — who you called, when, and what they said — so you're never re-explaining from scratch.

Staying Organized Without Feeling Overwhelmed

A one-page tracker works well: appointment type, who's responsible, confirmed date and time, location, and a "confirmed?" checkbox. Update it as calls come back. This turns a scattered list into something you can see at a glance during an already stressful week.
