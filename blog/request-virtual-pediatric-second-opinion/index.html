How to Request a Virtual Pediatric Second Opinion: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

August 26, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When your child is facing a big diagnosis or a treatment decision, wanting another set of expert eyes is completely natural. A virtual second opinion lets a specialist review your child's case remotely — often without long-distance travel — and it can be especially helpful when the specialty you need isn't nearby. This guide walks you through how to request one, step by step, so the process feels manageable instead of overwhelming.

Just to be clear: this is an organizing and preparation guide, not medical advice. Every clinical decision belongs with your child's licensed providers. Our goal is to help you understand the steps and gather what you need.

What Is a Virtual Pediatric Second Opinion?

A virtual second opinion is a remote consultation in which a pediatric specialist reviews your child's records, imaging, and history — and sometimes meets with you by video — to share their independent perspective. Some are "record-review only," where the specialist studies documents and sends a written summary. Others include a live video visit. Knowing which type a program offers helps you set expectations before you begin.

Step 1: Decide What You Want the Second Opinion to Address

Before you reach out to anyone, get clear on your goal. Are you confirming a diagnosis? Exploring whether other options exist? Understanding a recommendation you were given? Write down one or two specific questions. This keeps the process focused and helps whoever schedules the visit match you with the right kind of specialist.

Step 2: Find the Right Program or Specialist

You have a few common paths to a virtual pediatric second opinion:

  • Children's hospital second opinion programs — many academic and pediatric centers offer formal remote review services.
  • Your child's current specialist or pediatrician — they can often refer you to a colleague or center for another perspective.
  • Telehealth platforms that focus on expert consultations — some connect families directly with subspecialists.

When you contact a program, ask whether they treat your child's specific age group and condition category, whether visits are live or record-based, and how they deliver their findings.

How Do I Actually Request a Virtual Second Opinion?

Start by calling or emailing the program's intake line and saying plainly: "I'd like to request a virtual second opinion for my child." They'll typically walk you through their intake form. From there, the usual steps are: complete the intake paperwork, sign a records-release authorization so your child's documents can be sent over, submit the records (or have your current provider send them), and schedule the review or video visit. Ask upfront what the turnaround time is and who your point of contact will be if you have questions.

What Should I Ask About Cost and Insurance?

Costs vary widely. Some virtual second opinions are billed through insurance; others are flat, out-of-pocket fees. Before you commit, ask the program: "What is the total cost, is any of it billed to insurance, and will I get an itemized receipt?" If you're using insurance, check whether the reviewing specialist is in-network and whether a referral or prior authorization is needed. Keeping these answers in writing protects you from surprises later.

Step 4: Prepare for the Visit Itself

If your second opinion includes a live video appointment, test your device and internet connection beforehand, find a quiet space, and have your written questions ready. Keep a notebook open to jot down what you hear. Afterward, ask how and when you'll receive the specialist's written summary so you can share it with your child's care team.
