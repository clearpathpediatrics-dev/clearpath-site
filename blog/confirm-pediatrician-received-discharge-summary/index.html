Hospital Discharge & Coming Home

How to Make Sure Your Pediatrician Received the Discharge Summary After a Hospital Stay

September 10, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric discharge summaryhospital dischargepediatric care coordinationcoming home after hospitalparent health navigation

When your child is discharged from the hospital, one small document does a lot of heavy lifting: the discharge summary. It tells your pediatrician what happened, what changed, and what follow-up the hospital team recommended. The problem is that families often assume it lands in the pediatrician's inbox automatically — and sometimes it simply doesn't. Different record systems, faxes that fail, and busy transitions can all leave your child's regular doctor working with incomplete information.

The good news: confirming your pediatrician received the summary is something you can do calmly, in a few short steps, without needing any clinical knowledge. This is about organization and follow-through, not diagnosis.

Why the Discharge Summary Can Get Lost in the Handoff

Hospitals and pediatric offices frequently use different electronic health record systems that don't talk to each other. The hospital may send the summary by fax, through a health information exchange, or by mailing a copy home with you. Any of those channels can stall — a fax number may be outdated, the document may sit unrouted in a general inbox, or it may arrive days after your child's first follow-up visit.

That's why it helps to treat the discharge summary like a package you're tracking, rather than assuming it was delivered. A quick confirmation now can prevent your pediatrician from being caught off guard at the next appointment.

A Step-by-Step Way to Confirm It Arrived

Here's a simple sequence you can work through in the days after coming home:

  • Get your own copy first. Before you leave the hospital, ask for a printed or electronic copy of the discharge summary and any after-visit paperwork. Having it in hand means you're never dependent on a transfer that may not have happened.
  • Ask the hospital how and when they sent it. Ask the discharge nurse or medical records department whether the summary was sent to your pediatrician, by what method, and to which fax number or system. Write down the date.
  • Call your pediatrician's office to confirm receipt. A day or two later, call and ask the front desk or records staff whether the discharge summary from the hospital has arrived and been added to your child's chart.
  • Offer to bridge the gap. If it hasn't arrived, ask the office for the best fax number or secure email, then request that the hospital resend it — or forward your own copy if the office accepts records from parents.

What Should I Say When I Call to Check?

Keep it short and specific. You might say: "My child, [name and date of birth], was discharged from [hospital] on [date]. I'm calling to confirm you received the discharge summary and that it's in the chart before our follow-up visit." Ask the person to note who you spoke with and the date. If the summary isn't there, ask exactly where the hospital should send it. Being concrete makes it easy for the staff to help you quickly.

How Long Should I Wait Before Following Up?

A reasonable approach is to confirm within two to three business days of discharge, and always before your child's first follow-up appointment. If a follow-up visit is scheduled soon after leaving the hospital, confirm even sooner — you want the pediatrician looking at the same information the hospital team had. If the document still hasn't arrived after your first call, follow up again the next day rather than waiting a week.

Keep a Simple Record So Nothing Slips

Jot down a few notes each time you touch this: the date the hospital sent the summary, the fax number or system used, the date you confirmed with the pediatrician, and the names of the people you spoke with. This little log makes it obvious where things stand and gives you something to point to if the document goes missing again.
