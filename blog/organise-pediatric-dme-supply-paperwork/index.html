How to Organise Pediatric DME and Supply Paperwork: A Parent's Filing System That Works

October 25, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

If your child uses durable medical equipment (DME) or regular medical supplies, you already know the paperwork has a way of multiplying. Prescriptions, authorizations, delivery slips, warranty cards, reorder confirmations — it arrives by mail, email, fax, and text, often from several companies at once. When you're mid-reorder and can't find the one document someone's asking for, it's genuinely stressful. The good news: a simple, repeatable system can turn that chaos into something you can manage in a few minutes a week.

This guide walks you through organising the paperwork itself. It is not about deciding what equipment your child needs or how to use it — those are conversations for your child's licensed providers. This is purely about keeping your records findable, current, and ready.

Start by Sorting Paperwork Into Four Categories

Most DME and supply paperwork falls into four buckets. Setting up these categories first — physical folders, digital folders, or both — makes everything else easier.

  • Orders and prescriptions: the signed orders from your child's provider that authorize each item.
  • Authorizations and insurance: prior authorization approvals, denial letters, appeals, and coverage details.
  • Delivery and billing: packing slips, invoices, statements, and proof of what arrived and when.
  • Equipment records: model and serial numbers, warranty info, user manuals, and maintenance notes.

Name each folder the same way in both paper and digital form so your brain only has to remember one system. When a new document lands, you'll know instantly where it belongs.

Create a One-Page Supply Master List

The single most useful document you can build is a one-page summary of every piece of equipment and every recurring supply. For each item, note the description, the supplier's name and phone number, the account or order number, the quantity and reorder frequency, and the date of the most recent order. Add a column for the authorization expiration date if there is one.

Keep this list where you can reach it fast — pinned inside your main folder and saved as a file you can text or email. When a supplier calls, when a new provider asks what your child uses, or when you're racing to reorder before you run out, this page answers almost every question in one glance. Update it the moment anything changes so it never drifts out of date.

How Long Should I Keep Pediatric DME and Supply Paperwork?

As a general organising rule, keep anything tied to active coverage, warranties, or current equipment for as long as your child is using that item — and hold authorization and billing records for several years in case of a coverage question or billing dispute. Delivery slips for consumable supplies can usually be cleared out once the next order arrives and matches what you expected. When you're unsure, lean toward keeping insurance and authorization documents longer, since those are the hardest to recreate. If you'd rather not store everything on paper, scanning documents into your category folders lets you keep records without the clutter.

How Do I Keep Track of Reorders and Renewals So I Don't Run Out?

Build reminders around two different clocks. The first is your supply clock — set a recurring calendar alert a week or two before you typically run low, based on the reorder frequency on your master list. The second is your paperwork clock — set alerts 30 to 60 days before any authorization or prescription expires, because renewals often need provider or insurer action that takes time. Linking a calendar reminder to each line on your master list means you're never surprised by an empty box or a lapsed approval. Many families also keep a running note of who they spoke with, when, and what was promised, which makes follow-up calls far smoother.

Keep a Simple Contact Sheet

DME involves a lot of moving parts: the supply company, your insurer, and your child's prescribing provider all touch the process. Keep one contact sheet with names, phone numbers, fax numbers, account numbers, and portal logins. When something stalls, you'll know exactly who to call and have your details ready — which often shortens the whole process.
