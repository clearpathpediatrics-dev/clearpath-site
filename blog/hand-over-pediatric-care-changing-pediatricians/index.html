How to Hand Over Pediatric Care When You Change Pediatricians: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

September 14, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric care transferchanging pediatricianspediatric medical recordscare team coordinationpediatric care navigation

Whether you're moving across town, switching for insurance reasons, or simply looking for a better fit, changing pediatricians is a common and completely reasonable decision. The tricky part is making sure your child's history — every diagnosis, referral, medication, and note — actually follows them to the new practice. When the handover is smooth, your new pediatrician starts on day one already knowing your child. When it isn't, you can spend months re-explaining things and repeating tests.

Here's a practical, parent-friendly way to organize the transfer so nothing important gets left behind.

Start Before You Leave the Old Practice

The best handovers begin while you still have easy access to your current pediatrician's office. Before you finalize the switch, do a few things:

  • Request a copy of your child's complete medical record for yourself, not just a transfer between offices. You are entitled to your own copy, and having one means you're never dependent on two offices talking to each other.
  • Ask what format they can provide — patient portal download, secure email, printed packet, or CD. Portals often only show a summary, so specifically ask for the full record.
  • Confirm any pending items: lab results not yet back, referrals in progress, prior-authorization requests, or forms the office was completing for school or specialists.

Getting your own copy first is the single most protective step you can take. Everything else builds on it.

What Records Should You Actually Transfer?

You don't need to hand your new pediatrician a mountain of paper. Focus on the documents that let them safely pick up where the last provider left off:

  • Immunization/vaccine record
  • Growth charts and recent well-child visit notes
  • Current medication list and known allergies
  • Any active diagnoses and ongoing care plans
  • Specialist names, contact information, and recent referral letters
  • Recent labs, imaging reports, and screening results
  • Hospital discharge summaries, if any

Organizing these into one folder — digital or physical — makes your first appointment dramatically more useful. If your child sees specialists, list each one with what they treat so the new pediatrician knows who else is on the team.

How Do I Officially Transfer Records to a New Pediatrician?

Most practices require a signed release-of-information form to send records from one office to another. Here's the typical path: contact the new pediatrician's office and ask for their records-request or transfer form, complete it, and submit it to your old office (that's the one releasing the records). Some offices let the new practice request records on your behalf once you sign a release. Ask both offices how they prefer to handle it, and get a name and date for who is processing your request. Transfers can take a few weeks, so start early — and because you already requested your own copy, your child is covered in the meantime.

Do I Need to Notify My Child's Specialists?

Yes — this is the step families most often forget. If your child sees any specialists, their reports and referrals usually route back to the pediatrician of record. When you switch, let each specialist's office know the new pediatrician's name, address, and fax number so future results go to the right place. Otherwise, updates can keep flowing to a provider you no longer see. A quick call or portal message to each specialist saves a lot of confusion later.

Set Up the New Relationship for Success

Once records are in motion, prepare for that first visit like an introduction, not just an appointment. Bring your own record copy, a written summary of your child's history in your own words, and a short list of questions or concerns. If your child has ongoing needs, tell the new pediatrician which decisions are already settled and which are still open, so they understand the full picture. Ask how this office prefers you to communicate, request results, and handle after-hours questions — every practice runs a little differently.
