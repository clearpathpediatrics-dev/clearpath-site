Who to Call When a Pediatric Referral Has Gone Quiet for Weeks

October 13, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric referralspecialist referral follow-uppediatric care navigationreferral stuckpediatric scheduling

You were told your child needed to see a specialist. Weeks have gone by, your phone hasn't rung, and you're left wondering whether the referral is lost, stuck, or simply waiting on someone else. That silence is stressful — and it doesn't mean you did anything wrong. Referrals pass through several hands, and sometimes they stall quietly without anyone flagging it. The good news is there's a clear order of who to call to get answers.

This guide walks you through exactly who to contact, in what sequence, so you're not calling the same office twice or waiting on a callback that never comes.

Start With the Office That Made the Referral

Your first call should go to the office of the provider who recommended the specialist — usually your pediatrician or primary care office. They are the ones who generated the referral, and they can tell you whether it was actually submitted, where it was sent, and whether anything on their end is still pending (like a signature, a note, or a records attachment).

When you call, ask for the referral coordinator or the person who handles outgoing referrals, not just the front desk. Have your child's name, date of birth, and the approximate date of the appointment where the referral was discussed. Ask three simple questions: Was it sent? When? And to which specialist's office exactly?

Then Call the Specialist's Scheduling Line

If the referring office confirms it was sent, your next call is to the specialist's office — specifically their scheduling or intake department. Many families assume the specialist will call them, but some offices wait for the parent to reach out, or they have a backlog that delays contact.

Ask whether they received the referral, whether it's complete, and whether your child is on a waitlist or ready to schedule. Sometimes a referral arrives but sits because a required document or insurance detail is missing. Asking directly often surfaces the holdup immediately.

Who Do I Call First If I Don't Know Where It's Stuck?

If you have no idea where the breakdown happened, call the referring (pediatrician's) office first. They can confirm whether the referral ever left their system. If it did, you move to the specialist. If it didn't, you've found your answer in one call — the issue is on the sending side, and they can resend it while you're on the phone. Working in this order keeps you from guessing and saves you from leaving messages at the wrong office for days.

Don't Forget Your Insurance Plan

Some pediatric referrals require prior authorization from your insurance before the specialist can schedule. If both offices say they've done their part but nothing is moving, call the member services number on the back of your insurance card. Ask whether an authorization is required for this specialty, whether one was requested, and what its current status is. Keep the reference number from any authorization they mention.

What Should I Say So the Call Actually Gets Results?

Be specific, calm, and organized. Open with: "I'm following up on a referral for my child that was placed about [X] weeks ago and I haven't heard anything." Then ask for the status, the reason for any delay, and a concrete next step with a date — for example, "When should I expect a call, and who should I ask for if I don't hear back?" Write down who you spoke with, the date, and what they committed to. That record makes your next call far more effective if you have to escalate.

When to Loop in Help

If you've made all three calls and still can't get a straight answer, it may be time to escalate within the referring office — ask to speak with the office manager or a nurse line. Repeatedly hitting dead ends is exactly the kind of situation where an extra set of organized, persistent hands can help.
