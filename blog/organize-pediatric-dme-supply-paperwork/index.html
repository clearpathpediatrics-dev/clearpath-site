How to Organize Pediatric DME and Supply Paperwork: A Parent's System

September 17, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric DME paperworkmedical supply organizationdurable medical equipmentpediatric care navigationinsurance authorization records

If your child uses durable medical equipment (DME) or regular medical supplies, you already know the paperwork multiplies faster than anything else in your house. Prescriptions, prior authorizations, delivery slips, reorder forms, and letters of medical necessity all arrive from different companies at different times — and each one seems to matter at exactly the wrong moment. The good news is that a simple, repeatable system can turn that chaos into something you can actually find and use. This guide walks you through how to organize it all, so nothing lifesaving gets lost in a stack of mail.

Start by Sorting Your DME Paperwork Into Four Buckets

Before you buy a single folder, sort everything you currently have into four categories. These four buckets cover almost every document a DME or supply company will ever send you:

  • Orders and prescriptions — the written orders and letters of medical necessity from your child's providers that justify each item.
  • Authorizations and coverage — insurance approvals, denials, prior authorization numbers, and any appeal letters.
  • Deliveries and reorders — packing slips, shipment confirmations, reorder schedules, and rental agreements.
  • Contacts and account info — supplier phone numbers, account numbers, and your representative's name for each company.

Keep these buckets whether you go paper, digital, or both. The categories stay the same; only the container changes.

Build One Page Per Piece of Equipment

Complex kids often have several suppliers at once — one for feeding supplies, another for respiratory equipment, another for a wheelchair. The easiest way to keep track is a single summary page for each piece of equipment or supply line. On that page, note the item, the supplier, the account number, when it was approved, the reorder cycle, and the date the current authorization expires.

That expiration date is the detail parents lose most often, and it's usually why a shipment suddenly stops. Writing it on one page — instead of buried in a letter — means you can glance at your list and see what's coming due. Update the page every time something changes, and keep the old version behind it so you have a history if a company ever disputes what was ordered.

How Do I Keep Track of DME Reorders So Supplies Don't Run Out?

Pick one place — a wall calendar, a phone reminder app, or a single notebook — and log every reorder date the moment supplies arrive. When a shipment comes in, immediately set a reminder a week or two before you expect to need the next one, based on the reorder cycle your supplier gave you. Also note the last date your authorization covers, since coverage can lapse even when your child's need has not changed. Having reorder dates and expiration dates in the same place lets you call ahead instead of scrambling when the closet is empty. If your provider needs to send an updated order, requesting it early gives everyone time to process it.

What Documents Should I Keep for Each DME Item, and for How Long?

Keep the current order or prescription, the most recent authorization or approval letter, at least one recent packing slip, and any denial or appeal correspondence. Hold onto approval and denial letters for as long as the equipment is in use — they're the record you'll reference if coverage is questioned later. Rental agreements should be kept for the full rental period plus a few months after it ends. When a piece of equipment is fully replaced or no longer needed, you can move its file to a labeled "archive" folder rather than tossing it immediately, in case a billing question surfaces down the road.

Keep a Digital Backup You Can Reach Anywhere

Even the best paper file is useless if it's at home and you're at a specialist's office. Snap a photo of every key document — orders, authorization letters, account pages — and store them in one clearly named folder on your phone or a secure cloud account. Name files simply, like "Feeding-supplies-authorization-2024," so you can search instead of scroll. That way, when a new provider or supplier asks for proof of an existing order, you can share it in seconds.
