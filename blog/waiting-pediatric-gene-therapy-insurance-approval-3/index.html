What to Do While Waiting for Pediatric Gene Therapy Insurance Approval: A Parent's Guide

November 21, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

If your child's care team has recommended gene therapy and you're now waiting on insurance approval, the silence can feel agonizing. These authorizations are complex, high-cost, and often take weeks. The good news: there are concrete, useful things you can do during the wait that keep the process moving and keep you feeling less powerless. This guide focuses on organizing, preparing, and navigating — not on clinical decisions, which always belong to your child's licensed providers.

Confirm Exactly What Was Submitted and When

The first productive thing you can do is pin down the facts of the request. Call your child's specialty clinic or the hospital's financial navigator and ask: What was submitted? On what date? To which department at the insurer? Was it sent as a prior authorization, a predetermination, or a letter of medical necessity? Write down the answers along with the name of the person you spoke with.

Then call the member services number on the back of your insurance card and confirm the request actually arrived on their end. Ask for a reference or case number and the name of the review team handling it. Having matching facts from both sides prevents the most common cause of delay: paperwork that was "sent" but never logged.

Build a Simple Tracking File While You Wait

A waiting period is the perfect time to get organized so you're ready to act the moment you hear back. Keep one folder — paper or digital — with:

  • The submission date and case/reference numbers
  • A running call log: date, who you called, what they said, next step
  • Copies of any letters, forms, or denials you receive
  • Key contacts: the specialty clinic, the hospital financial counselor, and insurer case manager
  • Your plan's documents showing timelines for authorization decisions and appeals

This file turns a stressful, scattered process into something you can see at a glance. If a denial or a request for more information arrives, you'll already have everything in one place.

How Long Should a Gene Therapy Prior Authorization Take?

Timelines vary by plan and by whether the request is standard or expedited, but most plans publish specific windows in your benefits documents. Standard reviews often run a few weeks, while urgent or expedited reviews can be faster. Ask your insurer directly which timeline applies to your request and what date their decision is due. If that date passes without word, you have a legitimate reason to follow up and ask why a decision hasn't been issued — note that gentle, factual follow-up on a missed deadline is one of the most effective things a parent can do.

Can I Do Anything to Help the Approval Along?

Yes — mostly by making sure the reviewers have everything they need. Ask the submitting clinic whether the insurer has requested any additional records, test results, or documentation, and whether those have been supplied. Offer to help gather anything that lives in your own files. You can also ask your clinic whether a peer-to-peer review (a conversation between your child's physician and the insurer's medical reviewer) is available, and whether a case manager has been assigned. You cannot make clinical arguments yourself, but you can make sure the people who can are connected and have no missing pieces.

Line Up Your Backup Plans and Support

While you wait, it's worth quietly preparing for several outcomes. Ask the manufacturer whether they have a patient support or access program — many gene therapies do, and these teams often help families navigate coverage and timelines. Ask the hospital about financial counseling and whether a reapproval or appeal pathway exists if the first answer is no. Keep your child's regular care and appointments on schedule, and take care of yourself during a genuinely hard stretch.
