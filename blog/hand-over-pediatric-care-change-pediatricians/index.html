How to Hand Over Pediatric Care When You Change Pediatricians: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

October 22, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Changing pediatricians is more common than people think — you move, your insurance changes, your child's needs grow, or a relationship simply isn't the right fit anymore. Whatever the reason, the goal is the same: move your child's care from one practice to another without losing anything important along the way. A good handover means the new pediatrician starts with the full picture, not a blank page.

The good news is that a smooth transition is mostly about organization and timing. Here's how to approach it step by step so your child's care stays continuous.

Start Before You Leave the Old Practice

The handover actually begins at your current pediatrician's office. Before your last visit, do a few things while you still have easy access:

  • Request a complete copy of your child's records. Ask for the full chart, not just a summary — including visit notes, growth charts, immunization history, lab and imaging results, and any specialist referral letters.
  • Ask how records are released. Most practices require a signed release form and may take days or weeks to fulfill the request. Starting early avoids a scramble.
  • Confirm the format. Ask whether you'll receive a secure digital file, printed copies, or portal access, and whether anything can be sent directly to your new practice.
  • Settle open items. Note any pending referrals, lab results you're still waiting on, or prescriptions that will need continuity.

Keeping your own copy — not just relying on office-to-office transfer — means you're never stuck waiting on paperwork during an appointment.

What records should I request when changing pediatricians?

Request your child's complete medical record, which generally includes: the full immunization record, growth and development charts, past visit notes, allergy information, current and past medication lists, results of labs and imaging, developmental or hearing/vision screening results, and copies of specialist reports and referrals. If your child has an ongoing condition, also ask for any care plans or letters your pediatrician has written. Having all of this in hand lets your new pediatrician understand your child's history quickly and reduces the chance of repeated tests or missed details.

Build a One-Page Snapshot for the First Visit

A full chart is thorough but hard to read at a glance. For the first appointment with your new pediatrician, it helps to bring a short, parent-written summary that orients them fast. Think of it as a cover sheet, not a replacement for the records. Useful things to include:

  • Your child's current diagnoses or ongoing concerns, in plain language
  • Current medications, doses, and who prescribes them (as written by your providers)
  • Allergies and reactions
  • Names and contact info for any specialists already involved
  • Recent hospital stays, ER visits, or major procedures
  • Any upcoming appointments, referrals, or results still pending

This isn't about interpreting anything medically — it's simply organizing what your licensed providers have already told you so the new team can confirm and build on it.

Protect Against Gaps During the Switch

The riskiest window is the gap between practices. A few habits keep your child covered:

  • Don't fully leave the old practice until the new one is established. Keep your current pediatrician as the point of contact until your first new appointment is booked.
  • Plan ahead for prescription refills so nothing lapses during the transition. Your prescribing providers handle the clinical decisions — your job is to flag the timing early.
  • Transfer pharmacy and specialist contacts so everyone knows who the new primary pediatrician is.
  • Confirm records arrived. Call the new office a few days before the first visit to verify they received the file, and bring your own copy as backup.

How do I avoid a gap in my child's care during the transition?

Overlap the two practices rather than making a clean break. Keep your current pediatrician active until the first appointment at the new practice is scheduled and records have been confirmed as received. Handle refill timing and any pending referrals before you switch, and carry your own copy of the records to the first visit. If your child has a chronic condition or sees multiple specialists, let each specialist's office know who your new primary pediatrician is so communication keeps flowing to the right place.
