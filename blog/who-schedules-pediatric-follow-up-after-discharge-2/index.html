Who Schedules Pediatric Follow-Up Appointments After Discharge (And How to Confirm Them)

November 4, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

In the rush of bringing your child home from the hospital, you're handed a stack of papers, a list of follow-up visits, and a lot of relief. Then, a few days later, the question hits you: Did anyone actually schedule those appointments — or was I supposed to? It's one of the most common gaps families fall into, and it's completely understandable. Discharge is busy, and responsibility for scheduling isn't always spelled out clearly.

Let's walk through exactly who is usually responsible for setting up each follow-up visit, and how to confirm that every appointment your discharge team mentioned is truly on the calendar.

Who actually schedules follow-up appointments after a pediatric discharge?

There isn't one single answer — it depends on the type of appointment. In practice, follow-up visits after a hospital stay are usually handled in one of three ways:

  • The hospital or discharge planner schedules it before you leave. This is common for specialty follow-ups, post-surgical checks, or appointments tied directly to the hospital's own clinics. You may leave with a date and time already printed on your discharge paperwork.
  • The hospital gives you a name and number, and you call to schedule. This happens often with outside specialists, therapists, or your child's regular pediatrician. The expectation is that you make the call — but it's not always stated plainly.
  • Your pediatrician's office coordinates it. Sometimes the hospital sends records to your primary pediatrician, who then reaches out to set up the next visit or refers you onward.

The trouble is that these three paths can get blurred. A nurse might say "they'll call you," while the discharge sheet says "patient to schedule." When no one is sure, the appointment can quietly fall through.

How to confirm each appointment is really scheduled

The safest approach is to treat every follow-up as unscheduled until you have independently confirmed it. Here's a simple way to do that:

  • Make a list from your discharge paperwork. Write down every provider, specialty, or clinic mentioned, along with the recommended timeframe (for example, "within 2 weeks").
  • For each one, ask: who scheduled this? If you have a date and time in hand, call that office to verify it exists in their system — hospital-booked appointments occasionally don't transfer correctly.
  • If you don't have a date, call directly. Use the number on your discharge papers. Ask: "My child was recently discharged and was told to follow up with you. Is there an appointment on file, or do I need to schedule one?"
  • Confirm the referral and records arrived. Specialists often can't book until they receive a referral or hospital records. Ask whether they have what they need.
  • Get confirmation in writing. A patient-portal message, text, or email reduces the chance of a mix-up.

A: First, don't assume the gap is on you to solve silently. Call the discharging hospital unit or the number listed for discharge questions and explain that you're having trouble booking within the recommended window. They may be able to expedite the referral, suggest an alternative, or clarify the timeline. Keep notes of who you spoke with and when. Timing questions that feel clinical — like whether a delay is safe — should go to your child's licensed providers, not be guessed at.
