What Questions to Ask at Your Child's Pediatric 4-Year Well-Child Check

November 14, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

The 4-year well-child check is a big one. Your child is heading toward preschool or kindergarten, talking up a storm, and changing almost weekly. But these visits can feel rushed, and it's easy to walk out of the room realizing you forgot half of what you meant to ask. A short list of questions you've thought through ahead of time makes all the difference — you get clearer answers, and your child's provider gets a fuller picture of how things are going at home.

Below are the questions parents most often find helpful at a 4-year visit. These are meant to help you understand and organize what your licensed provider shares — not to replace their guidance. Jot down the few that matter most to you and bring them along.

Questions About Growth and Everyday Health

Growth and routine health usually open the visit, so it helps to have your observations ready. Consider asking:

  • How is my child's growth tracking compared to past visits, and is anything you'd like me to keep an eye on?
  • Are their eating and appetite patterns within a typical range for this age?
  • How much sleep is generally expected at 4, and does what I'm describing sound on track?
  • Is there anything about my child's vision or hearing you'd suggest we follow up on?
  • Are we up to date on everything recommended before preschool or kindergarten?

If you've noticed changes — picky eating, night waking, frequent complaints — mention them plainly. Specific examples help your provider respond to your actual situation rather than generalities.

Questions About Development and Behavior

At 4, providers often ask about language, play, motor skills, and social behavior. You can ask about these too:

  • Based on what you're seeing today, how is my child's speech and language developing?
  • Are there developmental milestones you'd expect around this age that I can watch for at home?
  • My child does (describe the behavior) — is that typical for this stage, or something to keep tracking?
  • How is my child doing with skills like dressing, following directions, and playing with other kids?
  • Is there anything you'd want to re-check at the next visit?

If something's been on your mind, this is the moment to say it out loud. You don't need the right medical words — describing what you see at home is exactly what helps.

Q&A: What Should I Ask If I'm Worried About My Child's Development?

Start by describing specific, concrete examples of what you've noticed and when — for instance, how your child communicates, plays, or handles transitions. Then ask directly: "Is this within the typical range, or would you recommend we look into it further?" and "If we wanted an evaluation, what would that process look like?" Asking these two questions helps you understand your provider's thinking and your options. Any decision about evaluations or referrals is always made with your licensed provider.

Q&A: What Questions Help Me Prepare for Preschool or Kindergarten?

Helpful questions include: "What forms or vaccine records will the school need, and can we complete them today?", "Are there any health or developmental things the school should know about?", and "Is my child's vision and hearing screening current for school entry?" Getting these confirmed during the visit saves you from a scramble later, and keeps your child's paperwork in one place.

How to Keep the Answers Straight Afterward

Visits move quickly, so capture answers before you forget. A few habits that help:

  • Write the provider's responses next to each question right in the room.
  • Note anything to "watch and revisit" at the next check-up.
  • Ask for any instructions or referrals in writing or through your patient portal.
  • Confirm when the next visit should be scheduled.

Keeping this in one running document means you walk into every future appointment already organized — which is exactly the kind of preparation that lowers stress over time.
