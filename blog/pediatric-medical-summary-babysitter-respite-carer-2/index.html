How to Prepare a Pediatric Medical Summary for a Babysitter or Respite Carer

October 21, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Leaving your child with a babysitter or respite carer can feel like a lot — especially when your child has medical needs, a daily routine, or instructions from several providers. A clear, written medical summary takes the guesswork out of those hours away. It isn't about handing over clinical decisions; it's about organising what your licensed providers have already told you so another caring adult can follow your child's existing plan confidently.

Here's how to build a summary that's calm, complete, and genuinely useful.

Start With the Essentials on One Page

The goal is a single page a carer can scan in seconds and reference in a hurry. If it's longer than that, people stop reading it. Keep it simple and put the most important things at the top.

Include:

  • Your child's full name, date of birth, and weight (some providers ask for weight; keep it current).
  • Your contact numbers and a backup contact if you can't be reached.
  • Your pediatrician's name and office number, plus any specialist your child sees regularly.
  • Known allergies exactly as your provider has documented them.
  • The nearest emergency room and your preferred urgent-care location, if your providers have noted one.

Write everything in plain language. A carer shouldn't need to decode abbreviations or guess what a note means.

Capture the Daily Routine and Medications as Your Provider Set Them

This is where most confusion happens. Rather than explaining why your child takes something, simply record what your provider already prescribed and when. List each medication by name, the exact time it's given, and how it's given — copying the instructions from the label or your provider's written directions word for word.

Do the same for routines: meal times, feeding methods, nap schedules, equipment, and any comfort items. If your child uses a device, a feeding pump, or a monitor, write down the normal routine and who to call if something isn't working — not how to troubleshoot it medically.

A helpful format is a simple time-based list: morning, afternoon, evening, bedtime. Carers follow a timeline far more easily than a paragraph.

What Should a Babysitter Medical Summary Include?

A strong pediatric babysitter or respite summary includes your child's identifying details, your contact information, the pediatrician and specialist numbers, documented allergies, a plain-language medication and feeding schedule exactly as prescribed, the daily routine, and a clear "who to call and when" section. It should also note where emergency instructions from your providers are kept, and any consent-to-treat forms your family uses. Keep it to one page when possible, and store a backup copy where the carer can find it.

Add a Clear "Who to Call and When" Section

Carers feel far more secure when they know the chain of contact. Spell it out:

  • Call you first for questions, changes, or anything that seems off.
  • Call your pediatrician's office for the situations your provider has told you to call about.
  • Call 911 for emergencies, following any instructions your providers have already given your family.

If your providers have written specific guidance about when to seek care, keep that document nearby and point to it — don't rewrite or interpret it. The summary simply tells the carer where to find your family's existing instructions.

How Do I Share the Summary Safely and Keep It Current?

Print a physical copy and post it somewhere visible, like the fridge, and keep a second copy with your child's bag. You can also send a photo to the carer's phone so it's always with them. Review the summary before each new carer and update it any time a provider changes a medication, routine, or instruction — even small changes. Date the page so everyone knows it's the latest version. Because it holds personal health details, share it only with people caring for your child, and ask carers not to forward it.

A Quick Pre-Handoff Checklist

  • Summary printed, dated, and posted.
  • Medications and routine match current provider instructions.
  • Contact numbers tested and correct.
  • Consent-to-treat form on hand if your family uses one.
  • You've walked the carer through the page in person.
