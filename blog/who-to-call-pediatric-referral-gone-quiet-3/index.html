Who to Call When a Pediatric Referral Has Gone Quiet for Weeks

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

You were told a referral was going in, and then... nothing. No call to schedule, no paperwork, no update. Days turn into weeks, and your child is still waiting to be seen. It's an exhausting place to be, and the frustrating part is that no one automatically tells you where things stand. The good news: there's a short list of people you can call, and calling them in the right order usually surfaces the holdup quickly.

This guide walks you through who to contact, in what order, and what to say so your call is productive instead of circular. The goal is simply to find the person who is holding the next step and ask them, kindly, to take it.

Start With the Office That Sent the Referral

Your first call goes back to your child's pediatrician's office, specifically the referral coordinator or front desk. They can confirm whether the referral actually left their system and where it was sent. Ask three plain questions:

  • Was the referral sent, and on what date?
  • Where was it sent — which specialist office or scheduling line?
  • Does it need insurance authorization, and if so, has that been submitted?

Write down the name of the person you speak with and the date. If they confirm it was sent, ask for a reference or authorization number. That single number makes every later call faster, because the next office can look you up instead of starting from scratch.

Then Call the Specialist's Scheduling Office

If the sending office says the referral went out, your next call is the specialist's office — specifically their scheduling or intake line. Referrals sometimes arrive and then sit in a queue waiting for review, missing information, or an available appointment slot. Ask whether they received it, whether anything is missing, and when scheduling typically happens for new patients.

If they have no record of receiving it, you've found the gap: the referral needs to be re-sent. Loop back to the pediatrician's office with that specific message — "the specialist has no record of it, can you please re-send and confirm the fax or portal went through?" Being that precise saves everyone another round of phone tag.

When to Bring in Your Insurance Plan

Many pediatric specialist visits need prior authorization before scheduling can happen, and a stalled authorization is one of the most common reasons a referral goes quiet. Call the member services number on the back of your insurance card and ask whether an authorization request has been received for your child, and what its status is. Have your child's member ID and the referral date ready.

Who Should I Call First If My Child's Referral Has Gone Quiet?

Call the office that created the referral first — usually your pediatrician's referral coordinator. They can confirm whether it was actually sent, where it went, and whether it needs insurance authorization. Starting there tells you immediately whether the holdup is on the sending side, the specialist's side, or with your insurance plan, so your next call goes to the right place.

How Long Should I Wait Before Following Up on a Referral?

A reasonable rule of thumb is to follow up if you haven't heard anything within about one to two weeks, and sooner if your provider described the referral as time-sensitive. You are never "bothering" anyone by checking in — referrals genuinely do get stuck, and a polite follow-up call is often what gets yours moving again. Keep a simple note of each call with the date and the person's name.

Keep a Simple Call Log as You Go

Each time you call, jot down the date, who you spoke with, what they said, and what the next step is. This turns a stressful back-and-forth into something you can actually track, and it means you never have to re-explain the whole story. If you need to escalate — to an office manager or a patient advocate at the specialist's practice — your log shows exactly what's already been tried.
