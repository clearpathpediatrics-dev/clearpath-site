How to Prepare a Pediatric Medical Summary for a Babysitter or Respite Carer

September 13, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Leaving your child with a babysitter or respite carer can feel like a leap — especially when there are medications, routines, or health needs to keep track of. A clear, one-page medical summary takes the guesswork out of it. It gives your carer the information they need to follow the plan your child's providers have already set, and it gives you a little more peace while you're away.

This isn't about handing over a medical file or asking a carer to make health decisions. It's about organizing what you already know so it's easy to find and easy to follow. Here's how to put one together.

Start With the Essentials on One Page

The goal is a single page a carer can scan in seconds and refer back to easily. Keep it clear and plain-spoken. At the top, include:

  • Your child's full name and date of birth
  • Your name and cell number, plus a backup contact if you can't be reached
  • Your child's pediatrician and any key specialists, with phone numbers
  • Preferred hospital or urgent care, as noted by your family
  • Insurance name and member ID, in case it's needed

Put the most time-sensitive information near the top. If your child has allergies or a condition that requires a specific routine, that belongs where no one can miss it.

List Medications and Routines Exactly as Your Provider Wrote Them

This is where accuracy matters most. Write down each medication using the exact name, amount, and timing your child's provider or pharmacy label states — don't paraphrase or estimate. Include when it's given (with meals, at bedtime) and how (with water, with food). If there's a device, equipment, or feeding routine, describe the steps plainly and note where supplies are kept.

Add daily rhythms too: nap times, meal preferences, comfort items, and what a typical good day looks like. Carers do better when they know both the medical details and the small things that keep your child settled.

What Should I Include About Emergencies and Warning Signs?

Include only what your child's licensed providers have already told you to watch for and what they've instructed you to do. Write it as a simple "if this, then this" list — for example, the steps your provider gave you and who to call. Note where any emergency medication or action plan is stored, and keep a copy of any written plan from your provider attached to the summary.

Always list 911 as the first call for a true emergency, followed by your cell and your backup contact. Avoid adding your own interpretations of symptoms; stick to the instructions your care team has given so the carer isn't put in the position of guessing.

How Detailed Should the Summary Be for a Short Visit?

Match the detail to the length and complexity of the visit. For a two-hour babysitting stint with a generally healthy child, a short version — contacts, allergies, one or two routines, and emergency numbers — is plenty. For overnight or multi-day respite care, or for a child with complex needs, expand it to cover full medication schedules, equipment steps, feeding, sleep, and behavioral cues. When in doubt, include a little more than you think you'll need, and highlight the truly critical items in bold so they stand out.

Make It Easy to Find and Keep It Current

A great summary is useless if no one can find it. Post a copy on the fridge, leave one in a labeled folder by the door, and text or email a digital copy to your carer ahead of time. Walk through it together before you leave so they can ask questions while you're still there.

Review the summary every few months, and update it right away whenever a medication, provider, or routine changes. Dating the page helps everyone know they're looking at the current version.
