How to Request the Written Pediatric Denial Reason and Policy Language From Your Insurer

October 30, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When a pediatric prior authorization is denied, the phone call or portal message you first receive is rarely enough to work with. Phrases like “not medically necessary” or “not a covered benefit” don’t tell you why, or which rule the insurer applied. To organize a strong appeal, you need two things in writing: the specific reason for the denial, and the exact policy language the insurer relied on. This guide walks you through how to ask for both, clearly and calmly.

Requesting these documents isn’t confrontational — it’s simply gathering the paperwork you’re entitled to. Having it in hand lets you and your child’s licensed providers see exactly what needs to be addressed.

Why the Written Reason and Policy Language Matter So Much

A verbal reason can be vague or incomplete. The written denial notice must state the specific basis for the decision, and most plans are required to tell you how to get the clinical criteria or coverage policy they used. Together, these documents reveal whether the denial turned on a missing document, a specific clinical criterion, a coding issue, or a benefit-coverage question. That tells you where to focus your appeal — instead of guessing.

Think of it as organizing: you can’t respond to a denial accurately until you know precisely what it says and which rule it points to.

What Exactly Should You Ask For?

When you contact the insurer, request these items by name:

  • The written denial notice (sometimes called an Explanation of Benefits, adverse determination letter, or notice of action).
  • The specific reason for denial — the exact wording, not a summary.
  • The medical or coverage policy (often called the clinical guideline, medical policy, or coverage criteria) the decision was based on, by policy name or number.
  • The appeal rights and deadlines that apply to your plan.

Ask for everything to be sent in writing — by mail, secure portal, or email — so you have a dated record.

How Do I Actually Request the Written Denial Reason?

Call the member services number on the back of your insurance card, or use your plan’s secure portal. Say something like: “My child’s prior authorization was denied. I’m requesting the written denial notice with the specific reason, and a copy of the medical policy or coverage criteria used to make the decision.” Then ask them to confirm how and when it will be sent.

Write down the date, the representative’s name, a reference or call number, and what they promised. If the request is easier in writing, send a short message through the portal or a dated letter or email so there’s a paper trail. Keep copies of everything in one place.

How Do I Get the Specific Policy Language They Used?

Ask the insurer to identify the policy by its exact name or number and to send you a copy of the full criteria. If they refer you to a public website, ask them to point you to the specific document that applied to this decision — policies get updated, so you want the version in effect on your denial date. If you can’t locate it, request it in writing and note that you’re asking for the exact criteria used in the adverse determination.

Having the actual criteria lets your child’s provider see which requirements were considered met or unmet — the clearest foundation for any appeal.

Organizing What You Receive

Once the documents arrive, keep them together with the original request. A simple setup helps:

  • Save the denial notice, the policy document, and your call log in one folder.
  • Highlight the exact sentence stating the reason, and the exact section of the policy it references.
  • Note the appeal deadline prominently so it isn’t missed.
  • Share copies with your child’s ordering provider so their team can respond to the specific point raised.
