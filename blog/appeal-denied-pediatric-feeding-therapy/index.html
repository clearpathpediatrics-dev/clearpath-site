How to Appeal a Denied Pediatric Feeding Therapy Request: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

August 28, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric feeding therapy appealdenied prior authorizationpediatric insurance denialfeeding therapy coveragepediatric care navigation

Opening a letter that says your child's feeding therapy was denied can feel like the floor dropped out — especially when you know how much your child needs support with eating. Take a breath. A denial is not a permanent "no." It's the start of a process, and that process is something you can organize and work through step by step. This guide walks you through how to prepare and file an appeal so your child's licensed providers and your insurance plan have everything they need to reconsider.

Start by Reading the Denial Letter Carefully

Every appeal begins with understanding exactly why the request was denied. Your denial letter (and the matching Explanation of Benefits) will include a reason and often a code. Common reasons include "not medically necessary," "missing documentation," "service considered educational rather than medical," or "out-of-network provider."

Write down or highlight the specific reason given. This matters because your appeal should respond directly to that stated reason. Also note two key details that are easy to miss: the appeal deadline (many plans require you to file within 30 to 180 days) and where and how to submit — by mail, fax, or an online portal. Missing a deadline is one of the most avoidable reasons an appeal stalls.

Gather the Documents That Support the Request

An appeal is stronger when it's backed by paperwork from your child's care team. You are not writing the clinical justification yourself — your child's providers do that — but you can be the person who collects and organizes it. Helpful items often include:

  • The original prior authorization request and the denial letter
  • Your child's evaluation results from the feeding or speech therapist
  • Notes or a letter of medical necessity from your child's licensed provider
  • Relevant records from your pediatrician or specialists
  • Your insurance card and policy or plan documents describing therapy benefits

Keep everything in one folder — physical or digital — with a simple cover sheet listing your child's name, member ID, and the claim or authorization number. A clean, complete packet makes it easier for a reviewer to say yes.

How Do I Actually File the Appeal?

Once your documents are together, follow the exact submission instructions in your denial letter. In general, the steps look like this: write a short cover letter that states you are formally appealing the denial, name the specific service (pediatric feeding therapy), reference the denial reason, and list the documents you're including. Ask your child's provider whether they will submit a letter of medical necessity to accompany your appeal — this provider-written explanation is often the heart of a successful case.

Submit through the method the plan requires, and keep proof: a fax confirmation, certified mail receipt, or a screenshot of your portal submission. Then log the date you filed and the date a decision is due (your plan will specify a response window). If you don't hear back in time, call and reference your submission proof.

What If the Appeal Is Denied Again?

If your internal appeal is denied, you typically still have options. Most plans allow a second-level internal appeal, and many situations qualify for an external review by an independent third party. Your denial letter should spell out these next steps and their deadlines. You can also ask your child's provider whether a peer-to-peer conversation with the plan's medical reviewer is possible. The key is to keep responding to the specific reason for denial and to keep your documentation organized and current.

Staying Organized Through the Process

Appeals can involve multiple phone calls, letters, and follow-ups. A simple running log — who you spoke with, the date, a reference number, and what was said — keeps you from repeating steps and helps you spot when a deadline is approaching. This kind of organization is exactly what tends to move an appeal forward, and it's the type of work ClearPath Pediatrics helps families structure so nothing slips through the cracks.

Remember: your role is to gather, organize, and communicate clearly. Clinical decisions and medical justifications stay with your child's licensed providers, and questions about your specific coverage should go to your insurance plan.
