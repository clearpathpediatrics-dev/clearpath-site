How Pediatric Private Duty Nursing Hours Are Approved for a Child: A Parent's Organizing Guide

August 24, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric private duty nursinghome nursing approvalprivate duty nursing hourspediatric home healthnursing authorization

If your child's care team has mentioned private duty nursing (PDN) — hands-on nursing hours provided in your home — you may be wondering how those hours actually get approved and who decides how many your child receives. The process can feel like a black box, especially when you're already managing a lot. This guide walks you through how approval typically works so you can understand the steps, organize your paperwork, and prepare for each stage. Every child's situation is different, and all clinical decisions belong to your child's licensed providers — but knowing the map helps you feel less lost.

What Private Duty Nursing Hours Actually Are

Private duty nursing refers to skilled, one-on-one nursing care delivered at home over extended shifts — very different from a short home health visit. Families often explore PDN when a child has ongoing needs that require nursing-level attention throughout the day or night. The number of hours a child receives isn't a fixed benefit; it's determined through a review process that weighs your child's documented needs against payer rules. That's why the paperwork and the story it tells matter so much.

How Are Private Duty Nursing Hours Approved for a Child?

Approval usually moves through a few recognizable stages. First, your child's physician documents the medical necessity — the clinical reasons nursing-level care is needed at home. From there, a home health or nursing agency completes an assessment and helps prepare the request. That request goes to your payer — private insurance, Medicaid, or a Medicaid waiver program — which reviews the documentation, often through a nurse reviewer or utilization team. The payer then issues an authorization for a specific number of hours over a set period. Hours are typically approved for a defined window (for example, several months) and must be renewed before they expire. If the request is reduced or denied, families generally have the right to appeal.

What Documentation Supports a Nursing Hours Request?

The strength of a request often comes down to how completely your child's needs are documented. Commonly requested items include:

  • A physician order or signed plan of care describing the skilled nursing needs
  • Recent clinic notes and hospital discharge summaries
  • A nursing assessment describing daily care tasks and their frequency
  • Any relevant equipment or supply needs already prescribed
  • Documentation of caregiver schedules or circumstances the payer asks about

You don't create these clinical documents yourself — your providers and the nursing agency do. But keeping organized copies means you can spot missing pieces, respond quickly to requests, and track deadlines. Missing or outdated paperwork is one of the most common reasons a request stalls.

How Long Does Approval Take, and Can Hours Change?

Timelines vary widely by payer and program, from a couple of weeks to longer for waiver-based programs that have their own steps. Approvals are almost always time-limited, so hours are re-reviewed at renewal. It's normal for the number of authorized hours to change over time as your child's documented needs change — sometimes up, sometimes down. If a renewal comes back with fewer hours than expected, that's typically the point where families gather updated documentation and consider an appeal. Marking renewal dates on your calendar well in advance helps you avoid a gap in coverage.

A Few Steps You Can Take Right Now

  • Ask your child's provider which program or payer the request will go through
  • Request copies of the plan of care and assessment for your records
  • Write down the authorization dates and set a renewal reminder
  • Keep a running list of questions for the nursing agency and your insurer

Understanding how the pieces fit together turns an overwhelming process into a series of manageable steps.
