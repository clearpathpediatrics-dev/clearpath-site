Switching Pediatricians? A Parent's Timeline for a Smooth Care Handover

September 15, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric care coordinationchanging pediatricianspediatric records transferpediatric care handoverparent health navigation

Deciding to switch pediatricians is a big step, and it can bring a lot of little worries with it. Will your child's history follow them? Will refills lapse? Will that specialist referral get lost in the shuffle? The good news is that a handover is far less stressful when you break it into a timeline instead of trying to do everything at once. Below is a calm, practical way to move your child's care from one practice to the next while keeping every important detail intact.

Start Before You Leave the Old Practice

The most common mistake parents make is choosing the new pediatrician first and worrying about the old records later. Instead, do a little groundwork while you still have an active relationship with your current office. That's when it's easiest to get answers, request documents, and clarify anything you're unsure about.

  • Ask the front desk how they handle records requests and how long transfers usually take.
  • Request a copy of your child's records for yourself, not just a practice-to-practice transfer, so you always hold a personal copy.
  • Note any outstanding items: pending lab results, an unfilled referral, a prescription that will need refilling soon, or a follow-up that was recommended but not yet scheduled.
  • Write down the names and contact details of any specialists your child sees, so the new pediatrician has the full picture.

Doing this early means you won't be chasing a former office after you've already moved on.

What Records Should You Actually Ask For?

You don't need to know medical shorthand to request the right things. A helpful transfer package usually includes:

  • The immunization record (often the single most-requested item).
  • Growth charts and well-visit summaries.
  • Recent visit notes and any hospital or specialist reports the practice has on file.
  • The current medication list and any active prescriptions.
  • Referral letters and results of any recent screenings or tests.

Ask whether records come through a patient portal, secure email, or paper. If your child is seen across multiple systems, gathering these in one place ahead of the first new appointment saves everyone time and prevents gaps.

Do I Have to Wait for the Offices to Transfer Records Themselves?

No. Many parents assume the two practices will quietly hand everything over behind the scenes, and while offices often do communicate, transfers can be slow, incomplete, or delayed by a signature that never got signed. You are entitled to request a copy of your own child's records directly, and having that personal copy means you're never dependent on one office's timing. If the new practice is missing something, you can supply it on the spot rather than waiting weeks. Think of yourself as the bridge between the two teams — the one person guaranteed to be in the room for every part of your child's care.

Set the New Pediatrician Up for Success

Once you've chosen the new practice, help their first visit count. Send or bring your records package in advance if you can, and prepare a short one-page summary in your own words: your child's ongoing concerns, current medications, allergies as noted by your providers, specialists involved, and any questions you want to raise. This isn't about diagnosing anything yourself — it's about making sure nothing important gets forgotten in a busy first appointment.

At the first visit, confirm three practical things: that they received the records, that any needed prescriptions can be continued without a gap, and how future referrals and results will flow to you. Ask how they prefer to be contacted between visits so you know the process before you need it.

Close the Loop

After the handover, do a quick check a few weeks later. Did the last refill go through? Did the specialist get the new pediatrician's information? Is the immunization record complete in the new system? Catching a small gap now is far easier than discovering it during an urgent moment.
