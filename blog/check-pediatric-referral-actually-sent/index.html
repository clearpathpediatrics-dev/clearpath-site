How to Check Whether a Pediatric Referral Was Actually Sent: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

October 13, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Your child's pediatrician said they'd send a referral to a specialist — but days or weeks have gone by and you've heard nothing. It's one of the most common (and quietly stressful) gaps in pediatric care: a referral that was intended but may never have actually gone out. The good news is that you don't have to guess. There's a clear way to confirm whether that referral was truly sent, and you can do it in a few focused calls.

Here's how to check, step by step, so your child's appointment doesn't stall before it ever starts.

Start by Knowing What "Sent" Actually Means

A referral usually travels through more than one stop, and "sent" can mean different things depending on who you ask. In most pediatric practices, the process looks something like this:

  • The pediatrician places the referral order in your child's chart.
  • Office staff send it to the specialist's office (by fax, electronic portal, or secure message).
  • If your insurance requires it, a separate authorization request may also need to be submitted and approved.

When you call to check, be specific: you're not just asking "did you refer us?" — you're asking whether it was actually transmitted to the specialist, and whether any insurance authorization was submitted. Those are two different things, and both can quietly get stuck.

Call the Referring Office First — and Ask These Questions

Your pediatrician's office is the best first call because they're the ones who sent it. Have your child's full name, date of birth, and the date of the visit ready. Then ask, calmly and directly:

  • "Was the referral to [specialist name or specialty] actually sent, or is it still pending in the system?"
  • "What date was it sent, and how — by fax, portal, or message?"
  • "Where was it sent? Can you confirm the specialist office name and fax or contact?"
  • "Did it also need insurance authorization, and if so, was that submitted?"
  • "Is there a reference or tracking number you can give me?"

Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the date. That record becomes incredibly useful if anything falls through later.

How Do I Confirm a Referral Was Received on the Other End?

A referral being sent is not the same as it being received. The only way to be sure is to call the specialist's office and ask them to look. Give them your child's name and date of birth and ask: "Have you received a referral for my child from [pediatrician's name]? If so, what date did it arrive, and what are the next steps to schedule?" If they have no record of it, ask the pediatrician's office to resend and request confirmation of successful transmission (many fax and portal systems generate a confirmation). This two-sided check — sent on one end, received on the other — is the single most reliable way to confirm a referral is truly in motion.

What If No One Can Confirm It Was Sent?

If the referring office can't verify it went out, the cleanest next step is to politely ask them to send it again and to confirm back to you once it's done. You can say: "I understand it may not have gone through. Could you resend it today and let me know once you've confirmed it was received?" If an insurance authorization is involved, ask when it was submitted and how you'll be notified of approval. Keep your notes in one place — dates, names, and reference numbers — so you never have to start the story over from scratch.

Keep a Simple Referral Tracking Record

A one-page log saves hours of repeated calls. For each referral, jot down: the specialist and specialty, date requested, who you spoke with, whether it was confirmed sent, whether it was confirmed received, and any authorization status. This turns a vague worry ("did that ever happen?") into a concrete checklist you can act on.
