Hospital Discharge & Coming Home

Questions to Ask the Pediatric Discharge Planner Before Your Child Comes Home

October 19, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Discharge day often arrives faster than you expect. One moment you're settling into the rhythm of the hospital, and the next a team is talking about going home. In that rush, it's easy to nod along and later realize you're standing in your kitchen unsure what to do next. The discharge planner is one of your best resources in that window — their whole job is to help you leave prepared. The key is knowing what to ask before you walk out the door.

This guide gives you practical questions to bring to that conversation. It won't tell you anything clinical about your child's care — that's for your child's licensed providers. Instead, it helps you organize, understand, and confirm what you're being told so nothing important slips through.

Questions About Medications and Instructions

Written instructions are only helpful if you actually understand them. Before you leave, ask the discharge planner to walk through the paperwork with you out loud. Helpful questions include:

  • Can you show me where the medication list is and confirm it matches what we'll pick up at the pharmacy?
  • Which prescriptions are new, which are continued, and which are stopped?
  • Are any medications hard to find, and do we need to call the pharmacy ahead?
  • Who do I call if the written instructions and what I heard verbally don't seem to match?

Ask them to clarify anything written in shorthand or medical terms. You are not expected to decode it alone — getting a plain-language explanation of what's on the page is exactly what this conversation is for.

Questions About Follow-Up Appointments and Who to Call

One of the most common gaps after discharge is the follow-up that never got scheduled. Pin this down before you leave:

  • Which follow-up appointments are already scheduled, and which do I need to book myself?
  • How soon does each one need to happen, and who makes those appointments?
  • Will my child's regular pediatrician receive the discharge summary, and when?
  • Who is my point of contact for questions in the first few days at home?
  • What number do I call after hours, and what counts as a reason to call versus wait?

Write down names and direct phone numbers wherever possible. "Call the hospital" is far less useful at 9 p.m. than a specific unit, clinic, or nurse line.

What Equipment or Supplies Will We Need at Home?

If your child is coming home with any equipment, supplies, or special feeding or care needs, ask who is responsible for setting that up and when it will arrive. Good questions include: Is the equipment being delivered, or do I pick it up? Who trains me on using it? What supplies do I need before we leave, and how do I reorder them? What happens if something doesn't arrive on time? Confirming these details at the hospital is far easier than scrambling once you're home.

What Should I Ask If I Feel Rushed or Unsure?

If the conversation feels too fast, it's completely okay to slow it down. You can say: "I want to make sure I understand before we go — can we go through this one more time?" Ask the discharge planner to put key instructions in writing, to repeat anything you missed, and to tell you exactly who to call if a question comes up later. There is no penalty for asking twice. A good discharge planner expects questions and would rather answer them now than have you leave uncertain.

Putting It All Together Before You Leave

Before you walk out, try to leave with four things clearly answered: what medications your child takes and how to get them, which appointments are scheduled and which you must book, what equipment or supplies are coming and from where, and who to call with questions. Jot the answers in one place — your phone or a single notebook page — so you're not digging through a folder later.
