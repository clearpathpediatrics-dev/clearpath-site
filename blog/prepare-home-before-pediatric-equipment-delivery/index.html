How to Prepare Your Home Before Pediatric Medical Equipment Is Delivered

September 19, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric DMEhome medical equipmentpediatric home nursingequipment delivery preppediatric care navigation

Bringing home a new piece of pediatric medical equipment — a feeding pump, oxygen concentrator, wheelchair, hospital bed, or bath chair — is a big step. The good news is that a little preparation before delivery day makes everything feel more manageable. This guide walks you through getting your home ready, room by room, so the equipment arrives to a space that's set up to receive it. None of this replaces the instructions from your child's licensed providers or the delivery company; it simply helps you organize what they've already told you to expect.

Start by knowing what's actually coming

Before you move a single piece of furniture, call the supplier and confirm the details. Ask what items are being delivered, roughly how large each one is, whether anything needs to be plugged in, and whether a technician will set it up or drop it off. Ask how many boxes to expect and whether any items require refrigeration or a specific temperature range.

Write these answers down. Knowing the footprint of each item — a stander takes up very different space than a suction machine — lets you plan realistically instead of guessing. If you're unsure who to ask, your care team or the discharge planner can point you to the right supplier contact.

Clear the path and the space

Delivery day goes faster when the route is clear. Walk the path the equipment will travel: front door, hallways, doorways, and the room where it will live. Move shoes, rugs, cords, and clutter out of the way. Measure doorways if you're expecting anything large, and note any tight turns or steps.

  • Entryway: Clear a straight path from the door inward.
  • Hallways: Remove tripping hazards and narrow obstacles.
  • Destination room: Decide exactly where each item will sit before it arrives.
  • Storage: Set aside a shelf, closet, or bin for supplies and boxes that come with it.

If your child's equipment needs to stay near their bed or a caregiver, plan for that placement now rather than shifting heavy items later.

Q: What do I need to check about power and outlets before equipment arrives?

Ask the supplier which items require electricity and whether any need a dedicated outlet or draw significant power. Then check that the destination room has enough accessible outlets near where the equipment will sit, so you're not relying on tangled extension cords. Note where your electrical panel is in case a breaker trips. Some families also ask their provider or supplier what a backup power plan should look like during an outage — that's a great question to raise before delivery, not after. Keep pathways to outlets clear and avoid overloading a single strip.

Think about cleanliness, safety, and other children or pets

Many pediatric supplies need a clean, dry, dust-free spot. Wipe down the surfaces and shelves where equipment and supplies will go. If you have younger siblings or pets, decide how you'll keep small parts, tubing, and cords out of reach. A latched cabinet or a designated high shelf can help.

Consider lighting, too. If you'll be checking or adjusting equipment overnight, a nightlight or reachable lamp makes those moments easier. Think about noise as well — some machines hum, so placement away from a light sleeper's headboard can help everyone rest.

Q: What should I have ready for the delivery technician?

Have a clear space cleaned and waiting, your confirmed delivery window, and a way to reach the supplier by phone. Keep a notebook handy to write down anything the technician demonstrates, and ask for written instructions and a contact number for questions later. If a parent or caregiver who will use the equipment daily can be present, that's ideal — they can ask questions in real time. Confirm who to call for repairs, resupply, and after-hours issues before the technician leaves.

Set up a simple supply station

Once the big items are placed, create one spot for everyday supplies: tubing, batteries, cleaning materials, and manuals. A single labeled bin keeps things findable when you're tired or in a hurry. Keep the user manual and supplier phone number somewhere both parents can reach.
