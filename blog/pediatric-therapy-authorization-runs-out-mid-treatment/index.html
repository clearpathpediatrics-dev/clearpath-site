What to Do When Your Child's Pediatric Therapy Authorization Runs Out Mid-Treatment

August 27, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric therapy authorizationprior authorization renewalpediatric therapy coverageauthorization expirationpediatric care navigation

Few things feel more frustrating than watching your child make real progress in therapy — speech, occupational, physical, or feeding — only to learn the authorization covering those visits has run out. If you've just been told your child's approved visits are used up or the approval window has closed, take a breath. This is a common, fixable paperwork situation, not a dead end. Here's how to organize your next steps so you can keep your child's care moving.

First, Confirm Exactly What Ran Out

"Authorization ran out" can mean a few different things, and knowing which one you're dealing with saves time. Start by gathering the facts before you make a single phone call.

  • Visit limit reached: The plan approved a set number of sessions (for example, 20) and your child has used them all.
  • Date range expired: The approval covered a window of time that has now passed, even if visits remain.
  • Re-authorization needed: The plan requires updated documentation to continue beyond an initial approval.

Look at your original authorization letter, your Explanation of Benefits, and any patient portal messages. Write down the authorization number, the exact expiration date or visit count, and the name of your child's therapy clinic contact. Having these details in one place makes every following step faster.

Contact the Therapy Clinic and Your Plan Right Away

Two calls usually get things moving. Call the therapy clinic first — many clinics track authorization status and submit renewal (re-authorization) requests on your behalf. Ask them: Has a renewal already been submitted? What documentation do you still need from us? What is the expected turnaround?

Then call the number on the back of your insurance card to confirm what the plan requires for continued therapy. Ask them to note the date and time of your call and the name of the representative. Keep a simple running log of every conversation — who you spoke with, what they said, and any reference numbers. That log becomes invaluable if timelines slip.

Can My Child Keep Attending Therapy While a Renewal Is Pending?

This is one of the most common questions parents ask, and the honest answer is: it depends on your plan and your clinic's policies. Some families choose to pause until the renewal is approved to avoid unexpected out-of-pocket costs; others continue with a clear understanding of what they may owe if approval is delayed or denied. This is a coverage and billing question — not a clinical one — so ask your therapy clinic's billing office directly what your financial responsibility would be during a pending renewal, and ask your plan whether retroactive approval is possible. Any decision about whether to continue treatment itself should be made with your child's licensed provider.

Gather What the Renewal Usually Requires

Re-authorizations almost always need updated documentation showing your child's progress and continued need. Your provider prepares this, but you can help speed it along by making sure the clinic has what it needs. Commonly requested items include:

  • A current progress note or updated evaluation from the therapist
  • Updated goals for the next treatment period
  • Any recent physician referral or order, if your plan requires one
  • Attendance history for the prior authorization period

Ask the clinic when they plan to submit and request a copy of the submission for your records. Note the date submitted so you can follow up if you don't hear back within the timeframe the plan quoted.

What If the Renewal Is Denied?

A denial is not the final word. Most plans offer an appeal process, and you have the right to a written explanation of why the request was denied. Ask the plan for the denial reason in writing and the deadline to appeal. Your child's provider can often add clarifying documentation, and in some cases a peer-to-peer review between your provider and the plan's reviewer is an option. Keep every letter, and note all deadlines on your calendar the moment you receive them.

Stay Organized So the Next Cycle Is Smoother

Therapy authorizations tend to renew on a cycle, so build a simple system now. Mark the new expiration date the moment you get an approval, and set a reminder several weeks ahead to check on the next renewal.
