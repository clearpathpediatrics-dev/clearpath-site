How to Keep a Pediatric Supply Inventory So You Never Run Out

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When your child relies on medical supplies at home — feeding bags, catheters, trach care items, dressings, syringes — running low is one of the most stressful surprises a parent can face. The good news is that running out is almost always preventable with a simple inventory system you can keep up without much effort. This guide walks you through building one that works in real life, not just on paper.

Nothing here is medical advice. It's about organizing and tracking what your child's licensed providers and suppliers have already set up, so the right items are always on hand.

Start by Listing Every Supply in One Place

Before you can track anything, you need a complete master list. Walk through where your supplies are stored and write down every item you use, no matter how small. For each one, note:

  • The exact name and size (copy it straight from the box or label)
  • The product or catalog number your supplier uses
  • Who provides it — pharmacy, DME company, or specialty supplier
  • How it's covered — insurance, secondary coverage, or out of pocket

Keep this master list somewhere both parents and any home nurses can reach — a shared phone note, a spreadsheet, or a printed sheet taped inside a cabinet. The exact format matters less than making sure it's the single source of truth everyone works from.

Set a 'Reorder Point' for Each Item

The heart of never running out is the reorder point — the quantity at which you place your next order, not the quantity at which you panic. To set it, you need two numbers: how many of each item your child uses per day or week, and how long it takes for a reorder to actually arrive.

A practical rule: reorder when you have enough left to cover the full delivery time plus a cushion of several extra days for holidays, backorders, or shipping delays. Write that trigger number right next to each item on your master list. When you count down to it, you order — no guessing, no 'I think we're fine.'

How Often Should I Count My Child's Supplies?

For most families, a quick weekly count on a set day works well — pick something easy to remember, like every Sunday evening. During that count, compare what's on the shelf to your reorder points and flag anything that's close. High-use items may need a mid-week glance. The goal is a rhythm you can keep consistently, because a simple system you actually follow beats a detailed one you abandon.

Keep a running 'reorder now' note during the week too. The moment you open the last box of something or notice stock dipping, jot it down immediately rather than trusting yourself to remember at count time.

What's the Best Way to Track Reorders So Nothing Slips Through?

The best method is one that closes the loop: record when you placed each order, what you ordered, and when it's expected to arrive. A dated log — on paper or in a shared app — lets you see at a glance whether a shipment is overdue so you can follow up before you're out. If a supplier promises a delivery and it doesn't come, your log gives you the order date and details to reference when you call.

It also helps to confirm each delivery against what you ordered, so a short or wrong shipment gets caught the same day instead of at your next count.

Build in Backups and Reminders

A few small habits make the whole system more forgiving:

  • Set calendar reminders for your weekly count and for recurring orders that ship on a schedule.
  • Keep supplier contact info attached to your master list so you're never hunting for a phone number in a pinch.
  • Note prescription or authorization expiration dates, since supplies can stall when a renewal is overdue.
  • Store an emergency cushion of your most critical items, and rotate it so nothing expires unused.
