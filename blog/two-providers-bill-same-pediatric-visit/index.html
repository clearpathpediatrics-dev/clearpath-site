What to Do When Two Providers Bill for the Same Pediatric Visit: A Parent's Guide

November 22, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

You opened the mail (or the patient portal) and found two separate bills for what felt like one single pediatric visit. It's a jolt — and it's more common than you'd think. The good news: in many cases this is sortable with a few organized phone calls and a careful read of your paperwork. This guide walks you through how to figure out what's really happening and what to do next, step by step.

None of this is medical advice. We're focused purely on the billing and paperwork side of things, so you can understand what you're being asked to pay and feel confident asking questions.

First, Figure Out If It's Really a Duplicate

Two bills for one appointment aren't always a mistake. During a single pediatric visit, your child may actually be seen or served by more than one billing entity. For example:

  • The physician or clinician who provided care bills under one tax ID.
  • The facility (hospital, clinic, or outpatient center) bills separately for use of the space and resources.
  • A lab, imaging group, or specialist consult may bill on its own, even if it happened in the same building on the same day.

So before assuming an error, lay both bills side by side. Look at the provider name, the service date, the billing address, and the procedure/CPT codes. If the codes and the billing entities are different, you may be looking at two legitimate charges for different parts of the same visit — not a duplicate.

How can I tell the difference between double billing and a legitimate second charge?

Compare the two bills against your Explanation of Benefits (EOB) from your insurer — not just the bills themselves. The EOB shows what each provider submitted, what insurance paid, and what you actually owe. If both bills list the same provider, same service date, and the same procedure code, and the EOB only processed that service once, you're likely looking at a true duplicate. If the EOB shows two different services (for example, an office visit code and a separate lab or facility code), the two bills are probably both valid. Your EOB is the referee here — always start with it.

Gather Your Documents Before You Call

A few minutes of prep makes these calls far shorter. Pull together:

  • Both bills (or screenshots from each portal).
  • The matching EOB from your insurance company.
  • Any after-visit summary or receipt from the appointment.
  • A pen and paper to log the date, who you spoke with, and a reference number for every call.

Keeping a simple running log is one of the most powerful things you can do. If a charge truly is a duplicate, you'll want a paper trail showing exactly who told you what and when.

Who should I call first — the provider or the insurance company?

Start with whichever party the EOB points to. If your EOB shows the service was only processed once but you received two bills, call the billing office that sent the duplicate first — ask them to review and explain each line. If the EOB shows the same service was processed twice, call your insurance company and ask them to reprocess, since a duplicate claim may have slipped through. When a provider and insurer disagree, ask each to put their explanation in writing, and don't pay a disputed charge until it's resolved. You can request that the account be noted as "in dispute" so it isn't sent to collections while you sort it out.

Steps to Resolve It Calmly

Here's a simple sequence to work through:

  • Confirm the facts using your EOB, as above.
  • Call the billing office and ask them to walk you through each charge line by line.
  • Ask directly: "Is this a facility fee, a provider fee, or a duplicate of a charge I've already been billed for?"
  • Request a corrected bill or reprocessed claim in writing if an error is confirmed.
  • Log every call with names, dates, and reference numbers.
  • Follow up in a week if you don't see a corrected statement.
