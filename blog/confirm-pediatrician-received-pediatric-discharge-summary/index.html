How to Confirm Your Pediatrician Received the Discharge Summary After a Hospital Stay

October 18, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric discharge summaryhospital dischargepediatric care coordinationcoming home from hospitalpediatric records

When your child is finally discharged from the hospital, you're focused on getting home, settling in, and following the instructions you were handed. One step is easy to overlook in the shuffle: making sure your child's pediatrician actually received the discharge summary. Hospitals usually say it will be "sent automatically," but that transfer doesn't always happen cleanly — and the pediatrician needs it to pick up where the hospital left off.

This guide walks you through how to confirm the handoff happened, so nothing falls through the cracks during those first days home.

Why the Discharge Summary Matters at the Pediatrician's Office

The discharge summary is the document that tells your child's pediatrician what happened during the hospital stay: the reason for admission, what was done, any medication changes, follow-up appointments, and what to watch for at home. Your pediatrician relies on it to support your child's ongoing care and to make sense of any new instructions you bring to the first visit after discharge.

Here's the catch: hospitals and pediatric offices often use different record systems that don't always "talk" to each other. A summary can get stuck in an electronic queue, faxed to an old number, or sent to the wrong office entirely. Confirming receipt is a small task that protects your child from gaps.

A Simple Step-by-Step Checklist to Confirm Receipt

You don't need to be technical or pushy to close this loop. Work through these steps in the first day or two after you get home:

  • Get your own copy before you leave. Ask the hospital for a printed or electronic copy of the discharge summary (not just the discharge instructions). Having it in hand means you're never dependent on a transfer that may or may not have happened.
  • Ask the hospital how and when it was sent. Confirm the method (electronic, fax, patient portal) and the date it went out, plus the name of the pediatric practice they sent it to.
  • Verify the destination details. Confirm the hospital has your current pediatrician's correct office name, fax number, or electronic address. Practices move and merge more often than you'd think.
  • Call the pediatrician's office directly. Ask the front desk or records staff whether a discharge summary from the hospital has arrived and been added to your child's chart. Give them the hospital name and discharge date.
  • Offer your copy as a backup. If they haven't received it, you can send or drop off your own copy through the office's secure method so your child's record is complete.
  • Note names and dates. Write down who you spoke with and when. If a follow-up is needed, you'll have a clear trail.

How Do I Know If the Pediatrician Actually Received It?

The only reliable way to know is to ask the pediatrician's office to confirm the document is in your child's chart — not just "in the system." A quick phone call to the records or front-desk staff, with the hospital name and discharge date, lets them look it up directly. If it hasn't arrived within a couple of business days of discharge, that's your signal to send your own copy and let the hospital know it didn't go through.

What Should I Do If the Summary Never Arrived?

If the pediatrician's office doesn't have it, start with your own copy — share it through the practice's secure portal, fax, or in person. Then contact the hospital's medical records department and ask them to resend it, confirming the correct destination this time. Keeping both the hospital and the pediatric office informed usually resolves it quickly. The goal is simply to make sure your child's pediatrician has the full picture before the first follow-up visit.

Staying Organized Through the Transition Home

The days after a hospital discharge are busy and tiring, so build a small folder — paper or digital — holding your copy of the discharge summary, medication lists, follow-up appointment details, and your notes on who you called and when. That one folder makes every future conversation with providers smoother.
