How to Ask for a Pediatric Developmental Referral at a Well-Child Visit: A Parent's Guide

October 6, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric developmental referralwell-child visitdevelopmental screeningpediatric care navigationparent checkup prep

If something about your child's development has been on your mind — the way they talk, move, play, or connect with others — a well-child visit is a natural place to bring it up. Many parents worry about saying it "the right way" or feel unsure whether their concern is big enough to mention. It is. Asking clearly and early helps your child's provider decide, with you, whether a developmental evaluation or referral makes sense. This guide walks you through how to prepare, what to say, and how to follow up — all as organization and preparation, never as medical advice.

Prepare Before the Visit So Your Concern Is Heard

Well-child visits move quickly, and it's easy to forget what you wanted to say once you're in the room. A little prep goes a long way. Before the appointment, jot down specific, concrete observations rather than general worries. Instead of "I think something's off," try notes like "at 18 months, uses about three words" or "doesn't point at things they want."

  • Write down 2–4 specific things you've noticed, with your child's age.
  • Note anything a teacher, daycare, or relative has mentioned.
  • Bring any screening forms or milestone checklists you've filled out.
  • Decide your top concern in case time runs short.

Having this written down means you can hand it over or read from it, even if nerves or a busy room make it hard to remember everything.

What Exactly Should I Say to Request a Referral?

You don't need medical language. Plain, direct words work best. Try something like: "I've noticed a few things about how my child is developing, and I'd like to talk about whether a developmental evaluation or referral would be helpful." Then share your written notes.

If the provider offers reassurance but you still feel uneasy, it's okay to ask directly: "I hear you, and I'd still feel better having this looked at. Can we put in a referral for a developmental evaluation?" You can also ask what the next step would be and whether a formal developmental screening can be done. Being specific — naming that you'd like a referral or evaluation — makes your request clear and easy to act on.

Ask These Questions Before You Leave the Room

Before the visit ends, make sure you understand what was decided and what happens next. Clear answers here prevent confusion later. Consider asking:

  • "Is a referral being placed today, or are we watching and rechecking?"
  • "Who will the referral go to, and will they contact me or should I call?"
  • "How long does this usually take, and what should I do if I don't hear back?"
  • "Is there anything I should document or bring to the evaluation?"

Write the answers down before you leave. If a referral is placed, ask for the name of the specialist or program and a timeframe so you know when to follow up.

What If My Provider Says to "Wait and See"?

Sometimes a provider may suggest monitoring development over the next few months rather than referring right away. That can be a reasonable clinical judgment, and the decision stays with your licensed provider. Still, you're allowed to ask questions. You might say: "What specific signs would tell us it's time to refer?" or "Can we set a date to recheck this?" Asking for a concrete recheck point turns "wait and see" into a plan you can track. If your concern grows before then, you can always call to revisit it.

Follow Up So the Referral Doesn't Fall Through

A referral request isn't finished until an appointment is actually on the calendar. Referrals can get delayed in busy offices or stall in insurance processing. A few days after the visit, call the office to confirm the referral was sent and ask who you should contact to schedule. Keep a simple note with the date you asked, who you spoke with, and the next step. This kind of tracking — the same organizing work ClearPath Pediatrics helps families with every day — is what keeps good intentions from slipping through the cracks.
