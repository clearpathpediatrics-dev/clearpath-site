Nurse Navigator vs. Case Manager vs. Social Worker in Pediatric Care: Who Does What

September 30, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric nurse navigatorpediatric case managerpediatric social workerpediatric care coordinationpediatric care team

When your child sees several specialists, you may start hearing new job titles: nurse navigator, case manager, social worker. They sound similar, they sometimes overlap, and it's easy to lose track of who to call about what. The good news is that each role has a distinct focus. Once you know the difference, you can aim your questions at the right person and save yourself a lot of hold music.

Here's a plain-language breakdown, written for a parent who is juggling appointments and just wants to know who does what.

What does a pediatric nurse navigator do?

A nurse navigator is typically a registered nurse who helps you move through a complicated system. Think of them as a guide for the clinical journey — not someone who diagnoses or prescribes, but someone who helps you understand the path your child's licensed providers have laid out and keeps that path organized.

A nurse navigator often helps you:

  • Understand what a referral is for and what to expect at the next visit
  • Keep specialists' instructions organized so nothing falls through the cracks
  • Prepare good questions before an appointment
  • Make sense of paperwork like visit summaries and referral orders
  • Know which provider to contact for which concern

Because this role is education- and organization-focused, it's the one that most directly reduces the daily mental load of managing complex care. A navigator won't tell you what a symptom means or change a treatment plan — that always belongs to your child's licensed providers — but they help you carry out and keep track of what those providers recommend.

What does a pediatric case manager do?

A case manager is usually focused on coordinating services and resources across the system, often with an eye on insurance, authorizations, and continuity of care. In many hospitals and insurance plans, the case manager is the person working behind the scenes to connect the moving parts.

A case manager commonly helps with:

  • Coordinating durable medical equipment, home health, or therapy services
  • Arranging authorizations and approvals through insurance
  • Planning transitions, such as from hospital to home
  • Connecting multiple providers so services are delivered in the right order

If a navigator is your guide, the case manager is often the logistics coordinator — the one making sure the approved services actually get set up. In some settings the same person wears both hats, which is exactly why families get confused.

What does a pediatric social worker do?

A social worker focuses on the whole family's wellbeing and the practical, emotional, and community supports around your child's care. They are trained to spot barriers that have nothing to do with the medical chart but affect everything anyway.

A social worker may help you:

  • Find community resources, support groups, or financial-assistance programs
  • Navigate school, housing, transportation, or food-access challenges
  • Process the emotional strain of caring for a medically complex child
  • Connect to counseling or family support services

Social workers are often the people who ask, "How are you holding up?" — and then point you toward real support.

How do I know which one to call?

Match the person to the problem. If your question is about understanding or organizing the clinical plan — what a referral means, how to prepare for a visit, who owns a follow-up — a nurse navigator is your best first call. If your question is about getting a service approved or set up — equipment, home health, insurance authorization — reach for a case manager. If your question is about emotional support or community and family resources, a social worker is the right door.

When you're not sure, it's completely fine to ask, "Who on my child's team handles this?" A good team will route you quickly, and you can write the answer down for next time.

Do these roles overlap?

Yes, and that's normal. Titles and duties vary by hospital, clinic, and insurance plan, so one organization's navigator may do things another calls case management. The practical move is to ask each person, early on, "What kinds of things should I bring to you?" Keep their names and direct numbers in your care binder so you're not rediscovering this during a stressful week.
