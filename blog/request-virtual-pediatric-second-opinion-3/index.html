How to Request a Virtual Pediatric Second Opinion: A Parent's Step-by-Step Guide

November 9, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When you're weighing a big decision about your child's care, the idea of a second opinion can feel both reassuring and overwhelming—especially if travel or wait times are already stretching your family thin. A virtual pediatric second opinion lets another specialist review your child's records and talk with you by video, often without leaving home. This guide walks you through exactly how to request one, step by step, so the process feels manageable instead of mysterious.

Remember: a second opinion is about gathering more information and clarity. The clinical decisions always stay with you and your child's licensed providers.

Step 1: Get Clear on What You're Asking

Before you contact anyone, write down the specific question you want answered. A focused request moves faster than a vague one. You might be asking for confirmation of a plan, a review of an upcoming procedure, or another perspective on next steps your current specialist recommended.

Jot down a few bullet points:

  • Your child's main diagnosis or concern (as your provider described it)
  • What your current specialist has recommended
  • The exact decision or question you're facing
  • Any deadline, such as a scheduled appointment or procedure date

This short summary becomes the heart of your request and helps the reviewing team know whether they're the right fit.

Step 2: Find the Right Virtual Second Opinion Program

Many children's hospitals, academic medical centers, and specialty clinics now offer formal virtual second opinion services. Start with these options:

  • Ask your current pediatrician or specialist for a recommendation—they often know respected programs and can help coordinate.
  • Check children's hospital websites for a "second opinion," "remote consultation," or "telehealth" program page.
  • Contact your insurance plan to ask which virtual second opinion services are covered and what documentation they require.

Write down the program's intake phone number, email, and any online request form. Note whether they serve your child's specific specialty and whether they accept out-of-state families, since telehealth rules can vary by state.

How Do I Actually Submit the Request for a Virtual Second Opinion?

Most programs have an intake step where you (or your referring provider) start the process. Here's the typical path:

  • Complete the intake form or call the program's coordinator. Share your short summary from Step 1.
  • Sign the medical records release so the program can obtain your child's imaging, labs, visit notes, and any pathology.
  • Confirm how the consultation happens—some are a written report, others include a live video visit, and some offer both.
  • Ask about cost and timing up front so there are no surprises.

Keep a running note of who you spoke with, the date, and what they promised next. That simple log prevents requests from quietly stalling.

Step 3: Gather and Organize Your Child's Records

The quality of a virtual second opinion depends heavily on the records the reviewing team receives. Having these ready speeds everything up:

  • Recent visit notes from the current specialist
  • Imaging (and the actual image files, not just reports, when requested)
  • Lab and test results
  • A current medication list with prescribers
  • A one-page summary of your child's history

If pulling all of this together feels like a lot, that's completely normal. This is exactly the kind of organizing and preparation work an RN care navigator at ClearPath Pediatrics can help you structure—so the reviewing team has a clear, complete picture from the start.

What Should I Ask During the Virtual Second Opinion Visit?

Go in with your questions written down, because video visits move quickly. Helpful questions to prepare include: Does the information confirm the current plan, or suggest other options to discuss? What would you want to know before deciding? What questions should I bring back to my child's current team?

Take notes or ask permission to record, and request a written summary afterward. Then share that summary with your child's regular providers so everyone stays aligned. The goal isn't to "pick a winner"—it's to give your family and your care team more information to decide together.

Keeping Momentum After the Request

Follow up if you haven't heard back within the timeframe you were given. A brief, friendly check-in email referencing your intake date usually does the trick. Keep every response in one folder so nothing gets lost between your current team, the second opinion program, and your insurance.
