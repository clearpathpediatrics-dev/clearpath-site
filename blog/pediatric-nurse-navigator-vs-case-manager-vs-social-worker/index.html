Pediatric Nurse Navigator vs Case Manager vs Social Worker: Who Does What

August 24, 2026 · 4 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric care coordinationnurse navigatorpediatric case managerpediatric social workercare team roles

When your child has multiple providers, appointments, and paperwork, you'll start hearing job titles that sound almost interchangeable: nurse navigator, case manager, and social worker. It's completely understandable to feel unsure about who to call when a question comes up. The good news is that each role has a distinct focus. Once you understand those differences, you can direct your questions to the right person and get answers faster.

Below is a plain-language guide to what each of these professionals typically does, how their roles overlap, and how to figure out who's who on your child's team.

What Does a Pediatric Nurse Navigator Do?

A nurse navigator is a registered nurse who helps you find your way through a complicated care journey. Think of them as your guide across the healthcare map. They tend to focus on the how and the what comes next of care.

Common things a nurse navigator helps with:

  • Explaining what a referral means and helping you understand the next steps your providers have outlined
  • Helping you prepare for specialist appointments and organize your questions
  • Connecting the dots between multiple providers so information isn't lost
  • Translating medical terminology into everyday language so you feel informed

Because navigators come from a nursing background, they're especially good at helping you understand the clinical picture your licensed providers have already described. They don't replace your doctors or make medical decisions — they help you carry out and keep track of the plan your providers created.

What Does a Pediatric Case Manager Do?

A case manager focuses on coordinating services, benefits, and logistics — often within a hospital, clinic, or insurance company. Where a navigator guides you through the care experience, a case manager tends to manage the moving parts behind it.

Case managers frequently help with:

  • Coordinating care between the hospital, home, and outpatient services
  • Arranging things like home health, durable medical equipment, or discharge logistics
  • Working with your insurance plan on covered services and prior authorizations
  • Making sure the pieces of a discharge or transition plan are actually scheduled

It's worth knowing that a case manager employed by your insurance company works within that system, while a hospital case manager works within the hospital. Their job is logistics and coordination — not diagnosing or treating.

What Does a Pediatric Social Worker Do?

A social worker focuses on your family's practical, emotional, and community needs. They look at the whole picture around your child's care — including the stress it places on your household.

Social workers often help with:

  • Connecting you to community resources, financial assistance programs, or transportation help
  • Providing emotional support and counseling referrals during difficult stretches
  • Helping you navigate school-related supports and paperwork
  • Addressing barriers that make care harder, like housing or food access

Social workers are trained to support families through the human side of a medical journey — the part that goes beyond charts and appointments.

How Do I Know Who to Call for a Specific Question?

A simple way to decide: match your question to the role's main focus.

  • Call the nurse navigator when you need help understanding the care plan, preparing for appointments, or connecting information between providers.
  • Call the case manager when your question is about logistics, coverage, home services, equipment, or discharge coordination.
  • Call the social worker when you need community resources, financial or emotional support, or help with barriers at home or school.

If you're not sure, ask any one of them — most will point you to the right teammate. It also helps to keep a short contact list of who fills each role on your child's team, along with their phone or portal information.

Do These Roles Ever Overlap?

Yes, and that's normal. In smaller practices, one person may wear several of these hats. In larger systems, the roles are more separate. The titles can also vary by organization — one hospital's "care coordinator" might do what another calls a "case manager." When in doubt, ask directly: "What kinds of questions are you the best person to help me with?" That single question clears up a lot of confusion.
