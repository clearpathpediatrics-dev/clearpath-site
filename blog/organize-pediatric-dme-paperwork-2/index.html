How to Organize Your Child's Pediatric Durable Medical Equipment (DME) Paperwork

August 1, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

If your child uses durable medical equipment (DME) — a wheelchair, feeding pump, standing frame, oxygen concentrator, hearing device, or specialty seating — you already know the equipment is only half the job. The other half is the paperwork. Prescriptions, letters of medical necessity, supplier orders, insurance approvals, delivery tickets, and repair records all pile up fast, and each one seems to be needed at exactly the moment you can't find it.

The good news: a little structure goes a long way. This guide walks you through organizing your child's DME paperwork so you can spend less time digging through folders and more time with your child. None of this replaces guidance from your child's licensed providers or DME supplier — it simply helps you keep what they've already told you in one calm, findable place.

Start by Listing Every Piece of Equipment

Before you sort a single page, make a simple master list of your child's current equipment. For each item, jot down a few key details you'll reference again and again:

  • The name and type of equipment
  • The prescribing provider
  • The DME supplier and their contact number
  • The date it was ordered or delivered
  • Whether it's a purchase or a rental
  • Any warranty or expected replacement timeframe

Keeping this list at the front of your binder (or pinned in a notes app) gives you an instant snapshot. When a nurse, therapist, or insurance rep asks a question, you'll have the answer without paging through everything.

Group Your Paperwork Into Predictable Categories

DME paperwork tends to fall into a handful of repeating buckets. Creating a labeled section for each — physical folders or digital folders both work — makes filing automatic:

  • Prescriptions and orders: the signed order or prescription for each item
  • Letters of medical necessity: documentation from your provider explaining why the equipment is needed
  • Insurance authorizations: approvals, denials, and any reference numbers
  • Supplier records: delivery tickets, invoices, and setup instructions
  • Maintenance and repairs: service dates, warranty info, and troubleshooting notes

Add a date to each document as it comes in. When something needs renewal or an item stops working, you'll be able to see the full history at a glance.

How Do I Keep Track of DME Renewals and Reorders?

Many pieces of pediatric equipment and their supplies need to be reordered or re-authorized on a schedule — often tied to a rental period, a supply cycle, or a required updated prescription. To stay ahead, create a simple renewal calendar. For each item, note the date the current authorization or prescription expires, then set a reminder several weeks before. That buffer gives your provider time to sign updated paperwork and your supplier time to process the order, so your child isn't left waiting. If your child is growing quickly, ask your provider or supplier how outgrown equipment is typically re-evaluated, and note those expected timeframes on your calendar too.

What Should I Do When Equipment Breaks or Needs Repair?

When equipment stops working, keep a short log of what happened: the date, what you noticed, who you called, and what they said. Contact your DME supplier first for repair or replacement steps, since they handle servicing and warranty claims. Keep any repair tickets and reference numbers in your maintenance section. If a repair affects your child's daily care, let your care team know so everyone is working from the same information. A written record also helps if you ever need to show a pattern of issues to your supplier or insurer.

Make It Easy to Grab and Go

Keep a lightweight "travel copy" of the essentials — the equipment list, current prescriptions, and key contact numbers — that you can bring to appointments, hospital stays, or emergencies. A single-page summary is often all a new provider needs to get oriented quickly. Update it whenever something changes, and store a digital version on your phone as a backup.
