What to Do While Waiting for Pediatric Therapy Insurance Approval

September 21, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric prior authorizationpediatric therapy insurance approvalprior authorization waitingpediatric care navigationinsurance appeals

Your child's provider has recommended a therapy, the paperwork is submitted, and now you're stuck in the hardest part: the wait. It's a stressful in-between, and it can feel like nothing is happening. The good news is that there's plenty you can do to stay organized, keep the process moving, and be ready the moment approval lands. Here's a practical, step-by-step way to spend the waiting period.

Confirm Exactly What Was Submitted and When

Before you can track anything, you need to know what's actually in motion. Call the office that submitted the request and ask a few simple questions: What was submitted? On what date? Was it sent as a standard or expedited request? Is there a reference or authorization number you can write down?

Getting these details in one place gives you a starting point. Note the name of the person you spoke with and the date of the call. If you keep a single running log for your child's insurance requests, this is the moment to add a new entry. When you call again later, you won't have to reconstruct the story from memory.

Know Who Owns the Next Step

Waiting feels worse when you're not sure who's supposed to act. Usually the request bounces between three parties: the provider's office (who submits and responds to requests for more information), the therapy practice (who may also submit their own paperwork), and the insurer (who reviews and decides). Delays often happen when the insurer asks for additional documentation and it sits in an inbox somewhere.

Ask each party the same question: "What are you waiting on, and what's the next thing that has to happen?" If the insurer is waiting on notes from the provider, you now know exactly where to nudge. This is one of the areas families ask ClearPath Pediatrics to help untangle, because a request can stall for weeks simply because no one realized the ball was in their court.

How Long Does Pediatric Therapy Prior Authorization Usually Take?

Timeframes vary widely by insurer, therapy type, and how urgent the request is marked. Many standard requests are decided within a few business days to a couple of weeks, while expedited requests may move faster. Your insurer's member services line and the plan documents are the most reliable sources for the specific timeline that applies to your child. Ask when you should expect a decision, and mark that date on your calendar so you know when to follow up rather than wondering day to day.

Set a Follow-Up Rhythm Instead of Waiting Passively

Rather than checking in randomly, build a simple rhythm. A reasonable approach is a short check-in call every few business days: confirm the request is still active, ask whether anything new is needed, and update your log. Keep each call brief and friendly. You're not being pushy — you're keeping your child's request from getting buried.

Keep these handy every time you call:

  • Your child's insurance ID and the reference number
  • The submission date and expected decision date
  • Your running log of previous calls
  • A pen to note names, dates, and any new instructions

Can My Child Start Therapy Before Insurance Approves It?

That decision belongs to your child's licensed provider and the therapy practice, not to us. Some families choose to ask the practice about self-pay options or a short trial while approval is pending; others prefer to wait. Ask the practice directly what their policy is, what any out-of-pocket cost would be, and whether starting early could affect the pending claim. Getting clear answers now prevents surprises later.

Prepare for Both Outcomes

While you wait, quietly get ready for either result. If it's approved, know how scheduling works and how soon you can book the first session. If it's denied, you'll want to be ready to move quickly — ask your plan how you'd file an appeal, what the deadline is, and what documentation tends to help. Having that information gathered ahead of time means a denial becomes a next step rather than a fresh crisis.

Keep copies of everything: the original recommendation, the submission confirmation, and any letters from the insurer. A tidy paper trail is your best friend if you ever need to appeal.
