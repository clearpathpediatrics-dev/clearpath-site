How to Share Pediatric Records Between Two Specialists So They Can Communicate

October 11, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric care coordinationspecialist communicationsharing medical recordspediatric navigationcare team coordination

When your child sees two specialists, you may assume they're comparing notes behind the scenes. Often, they're not — not because they don't care, but because they're on different systems, in different offices, and busy with full schedules. The good news is that you can set the stage for them to connect, without ever playing doctor or guessing at what any of it means clinically. Your job is to open the door and pass the right paperwork through it.

Below are practical, parent-friendly steps you can take to help two specialists reach each other — focused entirely on organization and communication, never on medical decisions.

Start by Giving Each Office the Other's Contact Details

The simplest reason two specialists don't talk is that they don't have each other's information. At your next appointment or phone call, hand (or read) each office the other provider's full name, clinic name, phone, and fax number. Ask the front desk a direct question: "What's the best way for Dr. A's office to send records to you?" Many offices still prefer secure fax or a patient portal, so it helps to know their preferred method before you try to connect them.

Keep a small running list of your child's providers — name, specialty, phone, fax, and address — so you can share it in seconds instead of hunting for it each time.

Sign a Release So Records Can Actually Flow

Even when two offices want to share information, privacy rules usually require your written permission first. Ask each specialist's office for a records release form (sometimes called an authorization to disclose or ROI form). Fill one out at each office, naming the other specialist as the person they're allowed to send records to. This single step clears the most common roadblock.

When you sign, you can specify what you want shared — for example, visit notes, test results, or imaging — so the exchange is focused and useful rather than a giant unsorted file.

Q: What's the fastest way to get two pediatric specialists to communicate?

The fastest route is usually to request records yourself and carry or forward them, rather than waiting for the offices to connect on their own. Ask Specialist A's office for a copy of the most recent visit summary and relevant results, then bring or send that copy to Specialist B before your next appointment. You become the reliable messenger. This doesn't replace direct provider-to-provider contact, but it ensures both have the same information quickly — and it often prompts the offices to reach out to each other once they see the connection matters to you.

Ask for a Direct Provider-to-Provider Conversation When It Matters

Sometimes passing paperwork isn't enough, and two specialists genuinely need to talk through a plan together. You can request this. At your visit, say something like: "I think it would help if you and Dr. B could discuss my child's care directly. Is that something your office can arrange?" Some practices will call or message each other; others may suggest a shared appointment or a coordinated plan. Either way, naming the need out loud makes it far more likely to happen.

If a referral between the two is appropriate, ask who is responsible for sending it and when, and get a timeframe so you can follow up if it stalls.

Q: What should I do if the specialists still aren't communicating?

If weeks pass with no contact, follow up in writing through each office's patient portal or by phone, and keep notes of who you spoke with and when. Confirm the release form is on file, confirm the fax or portal message was received, and ask for a specific next step. If coordinating multiple providers feels overwhelming, a care navigator can help you track releases, confirm records were received, and keep the thread moving — which is exactly the kind of organizing support ClearPath Pediatrics is built to provide.

Keep a Simple Paper Trail

Every time you request records, send records, or sign a release, jot down the date, the office, and the person you talked to. This running log protects you if something gets lost and makes follow-up calls much faster. You don't need anything fancy — a notebook or a notes app is plenty.

Getting two specialists connected is rarely one dramatic phone call; it's a handful of small, steady steps you can absolutely manage. If you'd like a hand keeping it organized, the team at ClearPath Pediatrics is here to help you navigate the logistics so you can focus on your child.
