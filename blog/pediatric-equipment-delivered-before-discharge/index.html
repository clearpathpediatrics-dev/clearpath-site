How to Get Pediatric Medical Equipment Delivered Before Discharge Day: A Parent's Guide

September 1, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

pediatric durable medical equipmenthospital discharge planningDME deliverypediatric home care suppliesdischarge preparation

Discharge day moves fast. Between paperwork, final instructions, and getting your child buckled into the car, the last thing you want is to arrive home and discover the equipment you were told about isn't there yet. The good news: with a little organizing ahead of time, you can help make sure the oxygen, feeding pump, monitor, or supplies your child's care team ordered are set up and waiting before you walk in the door.

This guide walks you through how to stay ahead of equipment and supply deliveries so you're not chasing anything on the day you're finally bringing your child home.

Start With the Equipment List From Your Care Team

Everything begins with knowing exactly what has been ordered. The company that delivers this gear is usually called a DME provider (durable medical equipment). Your child's discharge planner, case manager, or social worker typically places these orders during the hospital stay.

Ask your care team for a written list of every item being ordered, and for each one note:

  • The name of the item (monitor, pump, oxygen concentrator, wheelchair, supplies)
  • Which DME company is filling the order
  • Whether it's a one-time delivery or a recurring supply
  • The target delivery date
  • A direct phone number for the DME company

Keeping this list in one place—a notebook or a note on your phone—makes it far easier to follow up. At ClearPath Pediatrics, we often help families turn a scattered set of verbal instructions into a single organized tracking sheet so nothing slips through.

Confirm the Order Is Actually Placed—and Ask for a Delivery Window

An order being "written" is not the same as an order being "received and scheduled." A common gap is that the hospital sends the order, but the DME company needs insurance authorization or a signed prescription before they'll deliver.

Call each DME company a few days before discharge and ask:

  • Have you received the order for my child?
  • Is anything still needed from the hospital or my insurance before you can deliver?
  • What day and time window can you deliver and set up?
  • Will someone show me how to use the equipment, or is that scheduled separately?

Write down who you spoke with and when. If they're waiting on paperwork, loop your discharge planner back in right away so they can send it.

Q&A: What if the DME company says they can't deliver before discharge day?

This happens, and there are usually options. First, tell your hospital discharge planner or case manager immediately—delivery timing can sometimes affect whether your child is cleared to leave, so they need to know. Ask whether the hospital can provide a short-term "bridge" supply of essentials to cover the gap, or whether a different in-network DME provider can deliver faster. The key is to raise the concern early rather than on the morning of discharge, when there's less room to solve it.

Q&A: Do I need to be home for the delivery?

In most cases, yes—especially for equipment that requires setup and a hands-on teaching session, like feeding pumps or oxygen equipment. Someone who will be caring for your child should be present so the delivery technician can demonstrate the equipment and answer questions. If discharge day itself is chaotic, ask whether delivery can happen the day before, so the house is ready and you're not learning new equipment while also settling your child in.

Prepare Your Home Before the Truck Arrives

A little setup at home makes delivery day smoother:

  • Clear a clean, accessible spot near an outlet for anything that plugs in
  • Make sure there's a clear path through doorways for larger equipment
  • Have a place ready to store recurring supplies so you can see when you're running low
  • Keep the DME company's number posted somewhere visible for reorders and troubleshooting

Ask the delivery technician to walk you through how to contact them for repairs, replacements, and routine resupply. Knowing how to reorder before you run out prevents future stress.

Keep One Master Tracking Sheet

The single most helpful thing you can do is maintain one running list: what was ordered, who's delivering it, when it's confirmed, and what's still pending. Update it after every phone call. This turns a stressful guessing game into a checklist you can actually see progress on—and it gives you a clear script when you follow up.
