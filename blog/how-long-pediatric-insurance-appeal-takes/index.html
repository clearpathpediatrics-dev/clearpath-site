How Long a Pediatric Insurance Appeal Takes: A Parent's Timeline

September 22, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

When your child's care is stuck behind an insurance denial, one of the first questions on your mind is simple: how long is this going to take? The honest answer is that appeal timelines vary by plan type and by how urgent your child's situation is — but there are general windows most families can expect. Knowing them helps you plan, follow up at the right moments, and avoid feeling like your appeal has vanished into a void.

This is a general overview to help you organize and track your appeal. It is not legal or medical advice, and your specific plan documents and your child's providers are always the final word on your situation.

The Two Main Appeal Speeds: Standard and Expedited

Most insurance plans offer two appeal tracks. A standard (non-urgent) appeal is used when your child can safely wait for a decision. An expedited (urgent) appeal is used when waiting could seriously jeopardize your child's health — and it moves much faster.

As a rough guide for many plans:

  • Standard appeal of a service not yet received (pre-service): often decided within about 30 days.
  • Standard appeal of a service already received (post-service): often decided within about 60 days.
  • Expedited/urgent appeal: often decided within about 72 hours, sometimes sooner.

These are common benchmarks, not guarantees. Self-funded employer plans, Medicaid, and marketplace plans can each set their own rules, so your denial letter and plan handbook are the most reliable sources for your exact deadlines.

What Happens Before the Clock Even Starts

Here's what many parents don't realize: the official timeline usually begins when the insurer receives a complete appeal — not when you first started working on it. That means the time you spend gathering the denial letter, the written policy language, and supporting documentation from your child's providers happens before the 30-, 60-, or 72-hour window starts counting.

To keep things moving, it helps to know your submission deadline (often 60–180 days from the denial date), send your appeal in a trackable way, and confirm the insurer received it. Ask for a reference number and the name of the person or department handling it. Write down every date and every call — a simple running log saves you enormous stress later.

How Long Does a Pediatric Insurance Appeal Take?

A standard pre-service pediatric appeal is commonly decided within about 30 days, a post-service appeal within about 60 days, and an expedited appeal within roughly 72 hours when your child's health can't safely wait. Your exact timeframe depends on your plan type and is listed in your denial letter and plan documents. The clock typically starts when the insurer receives a complete appeal, so submitting everything at once helps avoid delays.

What If the First Appeal Is Denied?

Many plans include more than one level of internal appeal, and each level has its own timeline similar to the first. If internal appeals are exhausted, you may be eligible for an external review by an independent third party outside the insurance company.

External reviews also have timelines — often around 45 days for a standard review and 72 hours or less for an expedited one, depending on your state and plan. So while a single appeal might take weeks, a case that goes all the way through multiple internal levels and then external review can stretch across several months. Building that possibility into your expectations early helps you avoid feeling blindsided.

Can I Request an Expedited Appeal for My Child?

You can request an expedited appeal when waiting for a standard decision could seriously endanger your child's health or ability to function. Your child's licensed provider typically needs to support that the situation is urgent. If your request is granted, decisions usually come within about 72 hours instead of weeks. If the insurer declines to expedite, ask in writing why, and confirm your standard appeal is still moving forward on its normal timeline.

Staying Organized While You Wait

Appeals test your patience, especially when your child is the one waiting. A few habits make the wait more manageable: keep every letter in one place, note each deadline on a calendar, and set a reminder to follow up a few days before a decision is due. If a deadline passes with no answer, call and reference your case number — insurers are held to their own timelines too.
