What to Do When Pediatric Specialists Give Conflicting Advice About Your Child

October 11, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Few things are more stressful than hearing one pediatric specialist say one thing and another specialist say the opposite. You're left holding two sets of instructions and a lot of worry about choosing "wrong." Take a breath. Conflicting advice is common when more than one expert is focused on their own piece of your child's health — and it doesn't mean anyone made a mistake. Your job isn't to decide who's right. Your job is to organize the difference clearly and bring it back to the people who can sort it out.

This guide walks you through calm, practical steps to do exactly that — without giving any medical advice. Every clinical decision stays with your child's licensed providers.

First, Write Down Exactly What Each Specialist Said

Before you try to resolve anything, capture the conflict in plain words. Memory blurs fast when you're anxious, and "they disagreed" isn't specific enough to act on.

For each specialist, note:

  • Who said it (name, specialty, clinic)
  • When they said it (date of visit or call)
  • What exactly they recommended, in their words
  • Why — any reasoning they gave

Then write one clear sentence describing the conflict: for example, "Specialist A recommends continuing X; Specialist B recommends stopping it." Seeing it side by side often reveals that the "conflict" is actually two providers addressing different concerns — which is useful information for the next conversation.

Is It Really a Conflict — or a Gap in Communication?

Many disagreements come down to each specialist not knowing what the other said or recommended. One doctor may be working from older information, or may not have seen a recent test the other ordered.

Before assuming the advice truly conflicts, ask yourself: Does each specialist know what the other recommended? Have they seen the same records and results? Often the fastest path to clarity is simply making sure both providers are working from the same picture — and then asking them directly which recommendation takes priority for your child right now.

Who Should Break the Tie When Specialists Disagree?

This is the question most parents ask, so let's answer it directly.

Q: When two pediatric specialists give conflicting advice, who decides what to do?

A: You do not have to decide, and you shouldn't try to. The right move is to route the conflict to a quarterback — usually your child's primary pediatrician, or whichever provider is managing the overall picture. Share both recommendations in writing, state the specific conflict in one sentence, and ask: "Given everything, what do you recommend we do first, and why?" The pediatrician can weigh both specialists' input, request a direct conversation between them if needed, and give you one coordinated plan to follow.

How to Bring the Conflict Back to Your Care Team

Once you've documented both recommendations, reach out to your pediatrician's office (or the managing provider) through the patient portal, by phone, or at your next visit. Keep your message factual and short:

  • State the two recommendations plainly
  • Attach or reference the relevant visit summaries
  • Ask one clear question: which should we follow, and in what order?
  • Ask whether the two specialists can communicate directly if the answer isn't clear

Written communication is your friend here. It gives the provider something concrete to respond to and creates a record of the plan you ultimately agree on.

What to Do While You Wait for Clarity

Q: What should I do in the meantime if I'm not sure which advice to follow?

A: Don't guess, and don't quietly follow one specialist over the other without telling anyone. Instead, tell the care team you're holding on a decision until you have clarity, and ask what's safe to do while you wait. If your child's situation feels urgent or you're seeing concerning changes, contact your provider or emergency services right away — a coordination question should never delay care your child needs now.

Keep a Record of the Final Plan

Once your care team gives you a coordinated answer, write it down: what you'll do, who confirmed it, and when. If a recommendation changed, note that too. This becomes part of your child's running story and prevents the same conflict from resurfacing at the next visit.
