What to Write Down Before a Pediatric Well-Child Visit: A Parent's Prep Checklist

August 30, 2026 · 5 min read · ClearPath Pediatrics

Well-child visits move fast. You wait, you finally get into the room, and suddenly all those questions you meant to ask float right out of your head. The single best thing you can do is walk in with a short written list. It keeps you calm, helps you use your time well, and makes sure the things you've been wondering about actually get addressed.

Here's a practical, parent-tested guide to what's worth writing down before you go — no medical knowledge required, just a few minutes and a note on your phone or a scrap of paper.

The Basics to Jot Down First

Start with the simple stuff, because these are the details providers commonly ask and parents commonly blank on in the moment:

  • New questions or worries you've had since the last visit — write them in the order they matter most to you, so your top concern gets covered first.
  • Sleep, eating, and daily routine notes — anything that's changed or that you've been curious about.
  • Developmental milestones you've noticed (new words, movements, skills) or ones you're simply wondering about.
  • Behavior or mood patterns at home, daycare, or school that you'd like to mention.
  • Questions from a partner, grandparent, or caregiver who couldn't come along.

You don't need to interpret any of this or figure out what it means — just capture what you've observed. Your child's provider will take it from there.

Track the Practical Details Too

These items save time and prevent the "I'll have to check at home" moment that leads to follow-up phone calls:

  • Current medications, vitamins, and supplements — names and how often, exactly as your provider prescribed them.
  • Any specialists your child sees and the date of their last appointment, so everyone stays in the loop.
  • Recent visits to urgent care, the ER, or another clinic since your last well-child check.
  • Forms you need signed — school, sports, camp, or daycare paperwork. Bring the actual forms.
  • Insurance or referral questions you want to sort out while you're there.

Keeping these written in one spot means you're not digging through your bag or memory while your child squirms on the exam table.

How Far in Advance Should I Start Writing Things Down?

A few days ahead works well for most families. Keep a running note on your phone and add to it as questions pop up during the week — that midnight worry is much easier to capture in the moment than to recall later. The night before, take two minutes to reorder your list so your most important questions sit at the top. That way, if the visit runs short, the things that matter most to you are already covered.

What If I Have Too Many Questions for One Visit?

That's completely normal, especially for a first well-child visit or after a busy stretch. Number your questions by priority and mention up front that you have several, so your provider can help pace the conversation. If some questions don't fit, ask about the best way to follow up — a patient portal message, a nurse line, or a separate appointment. Nothing gets lost when it's written down; you can always circle back to what didn't get answered.

Organizing It All Without the Overwhelm

You don't need a fancy system. A running note titled with the visit date, a folded index card, or a page in a notebook all work. The goal is simply to walk in prepared and walk out with answers. If your family juggles multiple specialists, medications, or referrals, keeping these notes in one consistent place from visit to visit makes each appointment smoother.
